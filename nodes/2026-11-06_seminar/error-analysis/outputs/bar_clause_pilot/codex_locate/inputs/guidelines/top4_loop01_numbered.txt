0001	# Biomedical Annotation Guidelines
0002	
0003	## What to Annotate
0004	
0005	### 1. Annotate all **Specific Disease** mentions
0006	A textual string referring to a disease name may refer to a **Specific Disease** or a **Disease Class**.  
0007	- **Disease Class**: Mentions that could be described as a family of multiple specific diseases (for example, an inheritance-pattern category such as "autosomal recessive disease").  
0008	- **Specific Disease**: Mentions that can be linked to one specific definition that does not include further categorization.
0009	
0010	**Example**  
0011	> Diastrophic dysplasia is an autosomal recessive disease characterized by short stature, very short limbs and joint problems that restrict mobility.
0012	
0013	- **Annotate**:  
0014	  - "Diastrophic dysplasia" as **Specific Disease**  
0015	  - "autosomal recessive disease" as **Disease Class**
0016	
0017	Sections 1.1 through 1.10 refine this decision. Where they overlap with each other or with other sections, the Rule Precedence section governs.
0018	
0019	#### 1.1 Referential scope takes precedence over head-noun genericity
0020	A mention whose head noun is a generic disease-category term (e.g., "disease," "disorder," "syndrome," "condition," "infection"), regardless of morphology, number, or inflection, is annotated as **Specific Disease** rather than **Disease Class** when it refers to one individuated condition that the text is about. This applies when either:
0021	- a narrowing modifier anchors it to a single entity: an etiologic agent, a specific gene or locus, a specific organism, a specific anatomical site, or a subtype qualifier that picks out one recognized form of the disease (e.g., "classical" in "classical galactosemia," Example 15); or
0022	- it is framed as the patient's or case's diagnosis or clinical event (e.g., through a participial or "presenting with / diagnosed with / found to have / distinguished from other causes of" construction; see also Section 1.4).
0023	
0024	A modifier that names a category shared by many diseases does not anchor the mention. An inheritance pattern such as "autosomal recessive" or "X-linked" narrows the class but does not pick out one condition, and it is not a subtype qualifier. "Autosomal recessive disease" in the example above therefore remains **Disease Class**.
0025	
0026	**Do not apply this rule** when:
0027	- the mention denotes a heterogeneous umbrella spanning multiple distinct etiologies, signaled by quantification, enumeration, or a set-denoting construction (e.g., "a variety of …", or a list introduced by an exemplifying phrase such as "such as");
0028	- the mention is one item in a coordinated enumeration of conditions or features (e.g., "characterized by A, B, and C"), because enumeration signals a set rather than one individuated condition;
0029	- the noun is used in a generic or kind sense about diseases in general rather than about a particular case;
0030	- the mention is a non-disease finding;
0031	- the mention is anaphoric, meaning it points back to a condition named elsewhere (Section 1.7).
0032	
0033	In these cases the standard Disease Class rules apply, or the mention is left unannotated under "What NOT to Annotate," item 4.
0034	
0035	This rule decides between Specific Disease and Disease Class only. It does not re-label spans already annotated as **Modifier** (Section 3).
0036	
0037	**Etiology-anchored condition mentions (Principle 4).** A mention whose modifier is an adjective derived from a single named causal organism or agent (e.g., "streptococcal," "meningococcal"), combined with a generic condition head such as "infection," "disorder," or "disease," is **Specific Disease** when the mention names the condition itself and either (i) the condition is diagnosed in or present in a patient, or (ii) it is causally linked to another entity through a diagnostic or causal predicate. This applies the etiologic-agent route of the first bullet above to these heads. It does not apply when:
0038	- the mention describes a phenotype, symptom, or pathological finding, especially within a manifestation or "characterized by" list; such mentions remain **Disease Class** (Section 1.3);
0039	- the modifier names no single causal agent, or names several (broad categorical adjectives, or coordinated agents), so the mention is a true umbrella category;
0040	- the modifier is descriptive, temporal, or anatomical rather than etiological;
0041	- the modifier is an inheritance pattern (e.g., "autosomal recessive," "X-linked"). Inheritance qualifiers do not anchor a mention to one condition, although the case-level frames of this section still apply (Example 25);
0042	- the mention is a bare symptom noun in a presenting-feature list, which is treated under the manifestation-list convention.
0043	
0044	Umbrella and "X of Y" defaults of Section 1.6 are applied after this rule.
0045	
0046	- **Individuated (Specific Disease):** "The proband was diagnosed with an X-linked disorder." → "X-linked disorder" as **Specific Disease** (patient-diagnosis framing).
0047	- **Category (Disease Class):** "X-linked mental retardation syndrome" used as a family of conditions → **Disease Class** (Example 12).
0048	
0049	#### 1.2 Privative phrases that name a gene-product condition
0050	A privative or state head (a noun denoting absence, lack, or reduced amount or activity of a function, such as "deficiency," "absence," "lack," or "deficient activity") combined with a gene-product name (a full enzyme or protein name, or its symbol) is labelled according to how the gene-product name is attached.
0051	
0052	- **Fixed nomenclature compound → Specific Disease (whole string).** When the gene-product name is a premodifier attached directly to the state head, the compound is a single recognized condition, and the whole string is annotated as **Specific Disease**. Examples: "G6PD deficiency," "glucose 6-phosphate dehydrogenase (G6PD) deficiency," "C7 deficiency." Do not split off the head noun or the gene-product name alone.
0053	- **Free "of" or "in" complement → not a Specific Disease span across the preposition, unless Section 1.8(c) or Moderation Principle 5 (Section 1.5) applies.** When the gene-product name is the object of a free preposition, the Specific Disease label is not extended across the preposition into the molecular entity by default. The label is determined by Section 1.5 (post-nominal molecular complements, the span-boundary rule, and the patient-condition paragraph), Section 1.6 (named-entity versus category test), and, where the complement forms a recognized clinical condition, Section 1.8(c) (see also Section 1.9). Causal, characterizing, or mechanistic predicates alone do not convert such a phrase into a Specific Disease; recognition as a named clinical condition, or a patient-condition frame under Moderation Principle 5, does.
0054	
0055	**Do not apply the fixed-compound rule** when:
0056	- The privative element is an adjective modifying a phenotype, patient, or sample (e.g., "G6PD deficient phenotype"). Label this **Modifier** (Section 3.2).
0057	- The phrase reports a mechanistic, biochemical, or experimental quantity with no condition framing. Leave it unannotated ("What NOT to Annotate," item 7; Section 1.7).
0058	- The phrase already contains a disease-class head noun or an eponym. The standard disease-nominal rules govern these, and this rule does not add a new span.
0059	- The phrase is a coordinated composite of several conditions. Defer to **Composite Mention** conventions (Section 2 and Section 2.1).
0060	
0061	- **Example:** "Patients with deficiency of adenosine deaminase presented with recurrent infections." → "deficiency of adenosine deaminase" as **Specific Disease**: the enzyme complement forms a recognized clinical condition, and the phrase names that condition in a case-level frame (Section 1.8(c); Moderation Principle 5; Example 20). Because the whole string is a recognized condition, the span-boundary rule of Section 1.5 does not block the label. The compact fixed form "adenosine deaminase deficiency" is **Specific Disease** as a whole string. A mechanism such as "a deficiency of norrin within the developing retina" is not annotated (Section 1.5; Example 30).
0062	
0063	#### 1.3 Generic pathological-state descriptors as Disease Class
0064	A span is annotated as **Disease Class**, including any qualifier that modifies the state-denoting head, when:
0065	1. its head is a generic, non-named noun phrase denoting an abnormal state of the patient: a symptom, sign, or phenotypic-abnormality noun (qualified, established, or bare within a manifestation frame, as set out below), a pathological-state nominal describing a patient-level finding (optionally preceded by an abnormality qualifier), or a deficiency-state nominal; **and**
0066	2. relative to a named disease or disorder in the same clause or sentence, it functions as one of the following: a predicate complement (e.g., after "is characterized by," passive or copular); an item in a coordinated feature list or in a list of such complements; a consequence or manifestation argument (e.g., after a verb of result, a causal connective, or "secondary to"); or a parenthetical gloss on the disease's pathology that adds a feature or manifestation of that disease.
0067	
0068	Lexical disease cues such as "disease," "syndrome," "-osis," or "-opathy" are not required. The trigger is the syntactic role combined with the semantic class of the head. No word list is used.
0069	
0070	**Manifestation-frame details.**
0071	- The label covers the full phrase, including adjectival premodifiers of the head (e.g., "exudative" in "exudative retinal detachment").
0072	- Each coordinated item in a feature list is labelled independently.
0073	- A bare sign or symptom noun with no qualifier and no diagnostic status (e.g., "seizures," "hypotonia," "spasticity," "ichthyosis") is labelled **Disease Class** when it occupies one of the frames in item 2 (predicate complement, list item, or gloss). A bare sign noun is not labelled when no named disease in the same sentence supplies such a frame, unless a patient-presentation frame applies (Section 1.4; Moderation Principle 6).
0074	- Established disorder terms in a feature list (e.g., "mental retardation," Example 11) are **Disease Class**.
0075	
0076	**Deficiency phrases:** For a generic deficiency-state nominal, label only the state-denoting span. Do not label the molecular, cellular, or genetic entity that is the argument of the deficiency nominal. Where the argument is a gene product, Sections 1.2, 1.5, 1.8, and 1.9 apply instead, including the span-boundary rule of Section 1.5, the patient-condition paragraph of Section 1.5 (Moderation Principle 5), and the clinical-outcome paragraph below (Moderation Principle 8).
0077	
0078	**Process nominals:** Tissue-level, cellular, or developmental process nominals (e.g., "development," "accumulation," "tumorigenesis") describe biological processes and are excluded by "What NOT to Annotate," item 5 when they are neutral or normal. When such a nominal carries a deviation marker and occupies a characterizing or causal pathological-outcome frame, it is labelled **Disease Class** under the compositional form of the deviation paragraph below (Moderation Principle 2; Rule Precedence item 20). For example, "abnormal retinal vascular development" in "characterized by" is annotated, whereas "development" or "accumulation" with no deviation marker is not.
0079	
0080	**Measured quantities:** A measured biochemical quantity or activity of a gene product (e.g., "deficient activity of FALDH") is not labelled, even as one item in a "characterized by" list ("What NOT to Annotate," item 7; Rule Precedence item 13; Moderation Principle 2(c)). This is the exception to the list-item rule above.
0081	
0082	**Deviation-operator and compositional pathological-state phrases (Principle 3; Moderation Principle 2).** A noun phrase is annotated as **Disease Class**, including the deviation qualifier and any non-molecular complement, when it is framed as a pathological outcome or characteristic of a named disease or of a mutation. The frame may be characterizing (e.g., "characterized by," "associated with," "a feature of") or causal with a patient-level outcome (e.g., "results in," "leads to"). Two forms are covered:
0083	- **Deviation operator on a head.** A noun phrase whose head is a deviation-denoting noun (a shortfall, abnormality, or loss), or in which a deviation qualifier modifies an anatomical-structure head. No word list is used, so the paragraph applies to unseen deviation nouns and qualifiers.
0084	- **Compositional form (Moderation Principle 2).** A deviation-from-normal marker (an adjectival abnormality or excess qualifier, such as "abnormal," "aberrant," "excessive," or "massive" when it denotes pathological excess; or a privative or deficiency prepositional phrase) combined with a generic biological substrate (a physiological process, an anatomical structure, or a molecular entity). The trigger is the relation between the marker, the substrate, and the pathological frame. No word list is used, so the form applies to unseen qualifiers and substrates.
0085	
0086	It does not apply in the following cases:
0087	- **Process heads.** A deviation qualifier on a biological-process nominal is labelled only under the compositional form, when the phrase is the argument of a characterizing or causal pathological-outcome predicate (e.g., "abnormal retinal vascular development" after "is characterized by," Example 27). A process nominal with no deviation marker, or one that describes normal physiology, is not labelled ("What NOT to Annotate," item 5; Rule Precedence item 12).
0088	- **Molecular complements.** A deviation or deficit head that takes a gene, protein, enzyme, or other molecular complement is labelled under the compositional form only when the phrase is a clinical characterizing feature of the named disorder. It is not labelled when it is a measured value (below) or a mechanism introduced by a result or consequence construction (Section 1.5; Rule Precedence item 20(a)); a clinical pathogenic outcome of that kind is labelled under Moderation Principle 8 (below). Otherwise, deficit phrases follow the deficiency-phrase paragraph above, the span-boundary rule of Section 1.5, and Section 1.9. Examples: "deficiency of norrin" in a result construction (Example 30), and "absence of the FMR1 protein" in a mechanism frame (Example 40).
0089	- **Measured values.** Deviation terms that describe a measured value, assay result, or normal or experimental variation are not labelled, even in a "characterized by" list ("What NOT to Annotate," item 7; Rule Precedence item 13). The "deficient activity of FALDH" in Example 31 is such a case.
0090	- **Causal agents.** When the frame points to a causal gene, mutation, variant, alteration, or polymorphism, the phrase is not labelled ("What NOT to Annotate," item 8).
0091	- **Non-disease events.** Outcomes of non-disease events, such as ligand binding, activation, or conformational change, are not pathological outcomes and are not labelled (e.g., the Cdc42-binding sentence of the WASP abstract).
0092	- **Lexicalized names and umbrella heads.** Named disorders follow Sections 1 and 1.1 and Section 1.4 (Rule Precedence item 14). Umbrella heads (e.g., bare "complications," "abnormalities," or "deficiency") remain excluded ("What NOT to Annotate," item 4).
0093	- **Nosological heads.** Disorder nouns such as "infection" or "disorder" are not deviation-denoting and are governed by Section 1.1 (etiology paragraph) and the manifestation-list convention.
0094	
0095	**Moderation Principle 2 negative constraints.** The compositional form does not apply when:
0096	- (a) the condition is a lexicalized, self-contained name (e.g., "Coats disease"), which is labelled under Sections 1, 1.1, 1.8, or 1.10 as appropriate;
0097	- (b) the substrate appears without a deviation marker, or is described as normal physiology (e.g., "normal retinal vascular development," or "critical for normal retinal vasculogenesis," which is not annotated);
0098	- (c) the phrase describes an experimental manipulation, reagent state, expression in a cell system, or a measured activity or value, even when it appears in a "characterized by" list (e.g., "deficient activity of FALDH," or "a severe reduction of FALDH enzyme catalytic activity" in a mutation-expression sentence; Example 31);
0099	- (d) the span is a coordinated multi-condition mention, which belongs under Composite Mention (Section 2 and Section 2.1);
0100	- (e) the phrase is a gene-product deficit in a mechanism or result-introduced frame, which Section 1.5 governs (Example 30). This is the narrower rule. The "results in" trigger of this form does not reopen it for gene-product complements in mechanism frames; clinical pathogenic outcomes are addressed by Moderation Principle 8 below;
0101	- (f) the phrase is a causal agent (item 8) or a non-disease event (see above).
0102	
0103	**Do not apply this rule** when:
0104	- The span is a named disorder or Specific Disease-type mention, including eponym- or site-named conditions. Named disorders follow Sections 1 and 1.1. An established disorder term such as "mental retardation" in a feature list remains **Disease Class** (Example 11). A nosological-state span that is an identity alias or the sole defining phenotype is **Specific Disease** under Section 1.8.
0105	- The span is a parenthetical that meets the identity test of Section 1.8(a). Such a span is an alias, not a feature gloss.
0106	- The span denotes a normal physiological process, a normal anatomical structure, or a gene, protein, or other biological product, except where the compositional form applies to a deviation-qualified process or anatomical outcome, or the molecular-complement route applies to a characterizing feature (see above).
0107	- The span denotes an etiological agent (a mutation, variant, or exposure) rather than a state of the patient.
0108	- The span is a process nominal without a deviation marker, or one that describes normal physiology ("What NOT to Annotate," item 5).
0109	- The descriptor appears only as background mechanism with no stated link to a named disease within the same clause or sentence.
0110	- The state is a measured biochemical quantity or activity of a gene product, or a molecular complement of a state noun that describes a mechanism (Section 1.5). A molecular deficiency introduced by a result or consequence construction is not labelled here, except under Moderation Principle 8 for a clinical pathogenic outcome (below). A process or anatomical outcome in such a construction is labelled under the compositional form.
0111	- The qualifier is a standalone modifier that does not form part of the state-denoting noun phrase. If the state noun is privatively modified as a phenotype or patient descriptor (e.g., "deficient phenotype"), Section 3.2 governs.
0112	- The state noun is an attributive premodifier of a following disease head, in which case Section 3.1 governs (Modifier).
0113	- The head is an unqualified or unspecified umbrella term (e.g., "complications," "abnormalities," "problems," bare "deficiency"). These remain excluded by "What NOT to Annotate," item 4, even in trigger positions. For example, in "C7 deficiency associated with infectious complications," only "C7 deficiency" is annotated.
0114	
0115	**Causal condition-state phrases (Moderation Principle 8).** IF a noun phrase consists of a generic deprivation or shortfall head (a deficiency, deficit, or insufficiency noun) followed by an *of* or *in* complement naming a gene or gene product, AND the clause presents that state as the pathogenic outcome of a mutation, lesion, or disease process (a result- or cause-type predicate), THEN label the full head-plus-complement phrase as **Disease Class**. Exclude any appositive or parenthetical identifier that follows it (e.g., "(the protein product of the NDP gene)"), and do not label the gene or protein name alone. This is the gene-product extension of the deviation-operator paragraph (Rule Precedence item 25).
0116	
0117	Application order and exclusions:
0118	- Moderation Principle 5 (Section 1.5) is applied first. A state framed as the patient's condition, a clinical presentation, or the cause of a named disease is **Specific Disease**, not **Disease Class**.
0119	- Molecular mechanism or hypothesis frames remain unannotated. A result-type predicate inside such a frame describes a molecular or cellular event that is explicitly proposed or explained as a pathway step (e.g., "we suggest … which results in a deficiency of [gene product] within [tissue]," or "apparently suppresses transcription, resulting in absence of [gene product]"). Moderation Principle 2(e) and Section 1.5 govern these, and the "results in" trigger does not reopen them.
0120	- Disease-level clinical outcome statements, in which a gene-product deficit is presented as the pathogenic outcome of a mutation or disease process without a patient-condition frame, are **Disease Class** (Example 52).
0121	- Do **not** apply this rule when the construction denotes a measured quantity, concentration, or biochemical or enzymatic activity (e.g., "deficient activity of [enzyme]," or "reduction of [enzyme] catalytic activity" in an expression experiment), or when the complement names a gene or protein with no causal-outcome framing. Those spans stay unannotated (Section 1.7(b); "What NOT to Annotate," item 7).
0122	- Umbrella heads (a bare "deficiency") remain excluded by "What NOT to Annotate," item 4. Causal agents (the mutation or variant itself) are not labelled (item 8).
0123	
0124	- **Example (illustrative):** "Disorder X is characterized by mental retardation, seizures, and hypotonia." → "Disorder X" as **Specific Disease**; "mental retardation" as **Disease Class** (established disorder term, Example 11); "seizures" and "hypotonia" as **Disease Class** (bare sign nouns in a "characterized by" list; Section 1.4).
0125	- **Example:** "Coats disease is characterized by abnormal retinal vascular development, which results in massive intraretinal and subretinal lipid accumulation." → "Coats disease" as **Specific Disease**. The deviation-qualified process phrase and the excess-qualified outcome phrase are **Disease Class** under the compositional form (Moderation Principle 2; Example 27).
0126	
0127	#### 1.4 Referent over form for common-noun condition phrases
0128	A lowercase, non-eponymous common-noun phrase with *-itis* or symptom-like morphology (e.g., "retinal detachment," "pericarditis") is not labelled from its surface form, its lowercase spelling, or its position in a list. Its label follows its referent:
0129	
0130	- **Specific Disease** when the phrase names the individual patient's own diagnosed or presenting condition, meaning it is the argument of a case-level frame: presentation ("presenting with/as"), diagnosis ("diagnosed with," "found to have"), or differentiation ("distinguished from other causes of"). It is also **Specific Disease** when a subtype qualifier narrows it to one recognized form, or when it is an alias or naming frame under Section 1.8.
0131	- **Disease Class** when the phrase describes a disease type, grouping, or feature, meaning a phenotypic attribute in a "characterized by" list, a "feature of" construction, or a parenthetical gloss that adds a feature or manifestation of a named disease (e.g., "(exudative retinal detachment)" glossing the lipid accumulation of Coats disease; Section 1.3).
0132	
0133	**Generic condition nouns in patient-presentation frames (Moderation Principle 6).** A generic condition noun (a sign, symptom, finding, or broad condition category that is not a named disease, eponym, or acronym) that heads a noun phrase introduced by a patient-presentation frame is **Specific Disease** (instance-level), even when it is coordinated with other conditions or modified by an adjective. A patient-presentation frame is a participial or prepositional construction that attributes the condition to an individual patient subject (e.g., "The infant presented with seizures and severe hypotonia"; Example 50). Each coordinated head is labelled independently. The same kind of noun under a type-level predicate (a predicate describing a disease category's defining features or outcomes, such as "is characterized by," "is associated with," or "a feature of") or as a descriptive feature of a named disease is **Disease Class** (Section 1.3). The frame, not the coordination or the adjective, decides the label.
0134	- The rule does not apply to named diseases, eponyms, or acronyms, which remain **Specific Disease** in every frame.
0135	- It does not apply when the subject is a disease type rather than an individual, or when no governing predicate can be identified. In those cases, do not decide the label from list structure or modifiers alone; fall back to the entity definitions and the conventions of this section and Section 1.3.
0136	- A family history, or the condition of a relative, is not attributed to the patient subject. Such mentions are governed by Section 3.4(a) and Section 1.6(a).
0137	- A bare broad category noun (e.g., bare "cancer") that is the complement of a history, risk, or population head remains **Disease Class** under Section 3.4(a) and Rule Precedence item 21. This rule governs sign and symptom nouns, and modified condition nouns, presented as an individual patient's condition.
0138	- Coordinated modifiers that share one head form one **Composite Mention** (Section 2.1; Moderation Principle 7), not two presentation items.
0139	
0140	**Negative constraints:**
0141	- Coordination in a clinical list does not by itself make a term a category; the frame decides (Moderation Principle 6). Under a patient-presentation frame, coordinated items are **Specific Disease**. Under a characterizing or feature-list frame, they are **Disease Class** under Section 1.3.
0142	- Eponymous and acronymic disease names remain **Specific Disease** and are not reclassified by this section.
0143	- Eponym-anchored finding nouns presented as a patient's condition are governed by Section 1.9. Eponym-anchored finding nouns that name a condition by identity are governed by Section 1.10.
0144	- This section does not govern the boundaries of **Modifier** tokens (Section 3) or **Composite Mention** spans (Sections 2 and 2.1), which follow their own definitions.
0145	- Case-level and characterizing predicates are not interchangeable. "Results in," "secondary to," or "caused by" alone do not make a phrase a case-level frame; those constructions are governed by Sections 1.3 and 1.5.
0146	- **Alternative names.** A parenthetical, appositive, or alias-marked name that refers to the same condition as the named disease (the identity test in Section 1.8(a)) is a separate **Specific Disease** mention, handled like an abbreviation (Section 6). A running-text reuse of an alternative name for an already-named disorder is not separately annotated ("What NOT to Annotate," item 3). A parenthetical that adds a manifestation of the disorder rather than naming it is a feature gloss (**Disease Class**).
0147	- **Bare sign or symptom nouns.** A bare sign or symptom noun (e.g., "seizures," "hypotonia," "spasticity," "ichthyosis") in a manifestation frame (a "characterized by" list, a feature list, or a gloss on the disease's pathology) is **Disease Class** under Section 1.3. The earlier review flag for these cases is withdrawn. Outside such a frame, and outside a patient-presentation frame under Moderation Principle 6, a bare sign noun is not labelled. Established disorder terms (e.g., "mental retardation," Example 11) keep **Disease Class** in feature lists.
0148	
0149	#### 1.5 Post-nominal molecular complements of state nouns
0150	IF a noun phrase has a privative or state head (a noun denoting absence or reduction, such as "deficiency," "absence," "lack," or "deficient activity") followed by an *of* or *in* prepositional phrase whose object is a molecular entity (a gene, protein, enzyme, complement component, or variant) that describes a mechanism or a measured or observed entity, THEN do not extend a **Specific Disease** span across the preposition into that entity. The head-plus-prepositional-phrase string is not **Specific Disease** unless Section 1.8 or Moderation Principle 5 (below) applies. Tag **Specific Disease** only on a separately lexicalized disorder name, with the span limited to that name (e.g., "ADA-SCID" in "deficiency of ADA (ADA-SCID)").
0151	
0152	- A mechanism-describing frame (e.g., "results in a deficiency of norrin (the protein product of the NDP gene) within the developing retina") is left unannotated. The Section 1.3 deficiency trigger does not apply to it. A result or consequence construction blocks Section 1.8. This is the narrower gene-product rule that Moderation Principle 2 yields to (Rule Precedence item 20(a)). A clinical pathogenic outcome stated at the disease level is **Disease Class** under Moderation Principle 8 (Section 1.3); it is not a mechanism frame.
0153	- A measured or observed entity frame (e.g., "deficient activity of FALDH") is left unannotated (Section 1.7).
0154	- A nosological naming frame (e.g., "patients with deficiency of ADA," or a parenthetical or appositive that names the disorder) is not a mechanism. It is governed by Section 1.8 (and by Section 1.9 for patient-condition frames), and when Section 1.8 applies the whole span, including its complement or gloss, is **Specific Disease**. Moderation Principle 5 (below) gives the same result for patient-condition frames.
0155	
0156	**Does NOT apply when:**
0157	- The molecular term is a premodifier inside a compact, lexicalized disorder name, with no preposition between the entity and the head (a bracketed symbol may intervene). For example, "glucose 6-phosphate dehydrogenase (G6PD) deficiency" and "G6PD deficiency" remain **Specific Disease** as whole strings (Section 1.2, fixed compound).
0158	- The construction is adjectival or participial (e.g., "G6PD deficient"), which is governed by the Modifier definition (Section 3.2).
0159	- The prepositional object names an organism-level condition or a disorder rather than a molecular entity, since that is an independent disease mention.
0160	- The "of" introduces a disease compound rather than attaching to a state noun (e.g., "prevalence of glucose 6-phosphate dehydrogenase (G6PD) deficiency"). The compound is annotated on its own under Section 1.2.
0161	
0162	**Span boundary for deficit constructions (Principle 2).** When a deficit-type head (a noun denoting absence, shortfall, loss, or deficiency of a substance, such as "deficiency," "deficit," "absence," "loss," or "deficient activity") takes an *of* or *in* complement that names a biological molecule, gene product, enzyme, protein, or molecular component, including a bare symbol:
0163	- do not tag the deficit phrase or its molecular complement as **Specific Disease**;
0164	- tag only the disorder-naming head that the construction modifies, if one is present (e.g., in "[disorder] with deficiency of [molecule]," only the disorder head is tagged);
0165	- exclude pre-nominal modifiers attached to the deficit noun or to the molecular complement (laterality, zygosity, severity, or organ qualifiers) together with the deficit phrase;
0166	- causal or genotype cues (e.g., "caused by," "heterozygous," "carriers") do not license tagging the construction on their own. Moderation Principle 5 (below) is the exception for a cause-of-disease frame, and genotype words remain excluded pre-head modifiers.
0167	
0168	This span rule is the narrower condition for deficit heads with molecular complements, so it is applied before Section 1.8(b) and before Section 1.9. It does **not** apply when:
0169	- the head is itself disorder-naming (e.g., disease, syndrome, disorder, cancer, an eponym, or a disease abbreviation);
0170	- the whole string is a conventional lexicalized condition name that functions as a single disease label, including a recognized clinical condition under Section 1.8(c) (e.g., "patients with deficiency of adenosine deaminase," Example 20);
0171	- the Section 1.8(a) identity-alias route applies;
0172	- the complement denotes an anatomical site, cell type, or other non-molecular entity;
0173	- Moderation Principle 5 applies (a privative biomolecule phrase in a patient-condition frame, below).
0174	
0175	The span rule does not change **Disease Class** decisions (Section 1.6) or Moderation Principle 8 decisions (Section 1.3). It does not apply to pre-nominal fixed compounds such as "C7 deficiency" (Section 1.2), which have no prepositional complement.
0176	
0177	**Patient-condition frame for privative biomolecule phrases (Moderation Principle 5).** IF a nominal phrase contains:
0178	- (a) a privative state noun as its head, meaning a noun denoting absence, lack, or insufficiency of a function (e.g., *deficiency*, *absence*, *lack*, or a deficient state noun that names a clinical state);
0179	- (b) a prepositional complement introduced by *of* or *in*, with an optional internal determiner, whose object names a specific biomolecule (a gene product, enzyme, protein, or complement component), optionally with ordinal or descriptive modifiers; and
0180	- (c) a context that frames the state as the patient's condition, a clinical presentation, or the cause of a named disease (e.g., *patients*, *presenting as* or *with*, *diagnosed with*, or *caused by* / *responsible for* where the cause is a named disease or the patient's condition),
0181	
0182	THEN label the span from the privative head through the final token of the biomolecule name as **Specific Disease**. Pre-head intensifiers and pre-head nouns are excluded (e.g., *complete* in "a complete deficiency of X," or *enzyme* in "enzyme deficiency in X"). Parenthetical acronyms and abbreviations of the biomolecule, and appositive identifiers, are excluded and remain O. A biomolecule named without a privative head remains O, because the schema has no Gene/Protein class.
0183	
0184	- **Negative constraints.** Do NOT apply this paragraph when:
0185	  - the state noun is not privative (e.g., *reduction*, *increase*, *level*) or the phrase quantifies a measurement, including "deficient activity of [enzyme]" or "reduction of [enzyme] activity" inside a characterizing list (Rule Precedence item 13; "What NOT to Annotate," item 7);
0186	  - the context describes an in vitro, assay, expression, or mechanistic finding rather than a clinical condition, including hypothesis markers (e.g., "we suggest," "we infer") and experimental expression in cell systems;
0187	  - the state is an effect introduced by a result or consequence construction (e.g., "results in," "resulting in," "leads to," "secondary to"), because the state is then neither the patient's condition nor the cause of a named disease. Patient-level context elsewhere in the abstract does not reopen it (Examples 30 and 40). Clinical outcomes of this kind are addressed by Moderation Principle 8;
0188	  - the complement names a disease, phenotype, pathogen, or non-biomolecular entity, since those cases belong to lexicalized disease heads or other classes;
0189	  - the same span is already covered by a lexicalized disease head, to avoid duplicate labeling (e.g., a compact fixed compound under Section 1.2);
0190	  - the biomolecule is the sole focus with no privative head (it remains O);
0191	  - the privative element is an adjective modifying a phenotype, patient, or sample (Section 3.2 → **Modifier**);
0192	  - the phrase names a causal agent, such as the mutation or variant itself (item 8).
0193	- **Causal predicates.** Under condition (c), a causal predicate counts only when it makes the state the cause of a named disease or of the patient's condition (e.g., a diagnosis frame in which a named disease "is caused by" a deficiency of a protein). A causal predicate that makes the state the outcome of a mutation does not satisfy condition (c). Moderation Principle 8 governs that case (Section 1.3).
0194	- **Relation to the span rule.** Where this paragraph applies, the span-boundary rule above and Rule Precedence item 15 do not block the label. Section 1.2 (free complement) and Section 1.8(c) are read consistently with it.
0195	
0196	- **Example:** "The proband presented with a complete deficiency of factor VIII (FVIII)." → "deficiency of factor VIII" as **Specific Disease** (Example 49).
0197	
0198	#### 1.6 Named-entity versus category reference test
0199	IF a disease-denoting mention is either:
0200	- **(a)** a bare or unqualified category head noun naming a disease type (e.g., "ataxia," "neuropathy"), excluding the umbrella placeholders listed in "What NOT to Annotate," item 4; or
0201	- **(b)** a head noun followed by a free prepositional complement ("X of Y") whose complement names a substance, gene product, enzyme, or measured variable rather than a recognized condition,
0202	
0203	THEN assign **Disease Class**, because the mention refers to a generic category, phenotype, or pathophysiological state.
0204	
0205	**Specific Disease** is assigned to such a mention only when it individuates one recognized condition through (i) an eponym, (ii) an acronym of a named disease, (iii) a fixed nomenclature compound in which the modifier is attached directly to the head rather than linked by a free preposition, or (iv) a nosological naming frame under Section 1.8, in which the complement forms a recognized clinical condition. Fixed "[gene product] deficiency" compounds (e.g., "C7 deficiency," "G6PD deficiency," "adenosine deaminase deficiency") are instances of (iii). Fixed site-plus-category compounds such as "ovarian cancer" are instances of (iii).
0206	
0207	**Scope and exclusions:**
0208	- The restriction "Specific Disease only when…" applies to mentions that match (a) or (b). It does not override the patient-diagnosis assignments of Section 1.1 (e.g., "X-linked disorder" in "diagnosed with an X-linked disorder," Example 25), because an inheritance qualifier makes the head qualified, not bare, and it is not a free "X of Y" complement.
0209	- A category noun that is an established component of a proper name or fixed term (e.g., "[Eponym] syndrome," "[Acronym] disease") remains **Specific Disease** regardless of how generic the head noun looks in isolation.
0210	- Section 1.8, Section 1.9, and Section 1.10 are applied before this section. A bare category head used as an alias or in a naming frame under Section 1.8 (e.g., "retinal telangiectasis" in an alias construction) is **Specific Disease**, not **Disease Class**.
0211	- The Disease Class trigger in (b) applies only where the complement is not a recognized condition (Section 1.8(c)).
0212	- Moderation Principle 5 patient-condition frames (Section 1.5; Rule Precedence item 22) are **Specific Disease** and are not caught by (b), because in that frame the complement forms a recognized condition.
0213	- A bare generic category noun in a prepositional complement of a history or risk head (e.g., "a family history of cancer") is **Disease Class** under (a) and Section 3.4(a), because its complement is not a named type.
0214	- This rule does not apply to mentions that lack a disease-denoting head and are not classified as disease entities. Those are governed by the Modifier and Composite Mention definitions.
0215	- This rule does not apply to mechanism or finding frames (Section 1.5), to Section 1.9 patient-condition frames, to Section 1.10 identity labels, or to anaphoric mentions (Section 1.7).
0216	- This rule does not override the Specific Disease definition for fixed compounds that name a specific enzyme or gene deficiency and are recognized as a single disease (Section 1.2). The distinction depends on whether the modifier is a fixed nomenclature element or a free complement, not on the presence of the head noun itself.
0217	
0218	#### 1.7 Name-versus-reference gate
0219	IF a candidate span's disease meaning comes only from:
0220	- **(a)** a generic or anaphoric head, meaning a determiner or demonstrative plus a disease-semantic noun ("this condition," "such disorder") that points back to an antecedent; or
0221	- **(b)** a descriptive finding, meaning a state or absence noun with a prepositional complement that reports an observed or measured entity or quantity in a sample, tissue, or mechanism ("absence of [molecule]," "lack of [component]," "deficient activity of [enzyme]," "reduction of [enzyme] activity"),
0222	
0223	THEN do not label the span **Specific Disease**. Leave it unannotated, because the disease is named elsewhere (case a) or the span reports a finding rather than naming a disease (case b). A patient-population diagnostic state that names a condition (e.g., "patients with deficiency of ADA") is not a finding under case (b); it is governed by Section 1.8(c), Section 1.9, Section 1.6, and the patient-condition frame of Moderation Principle 5 (Section 1.5).
0224	
0225	**Test:** Can the span be read as a self-contained disease name without resolving a pronoun or interpreting a clinical finding? If not, it is not **Specific Disease**.
0226	
0227	**Negative constraints:**
0228	- This rule does NOT apply when the span is a lexicalized disease name, even if its head noun is generic. This includes eponyms, disease acronyms, and fixed compound labels such as "[X] deficiency," "[X] syndrome," or "[X] cancer."
0229	- This rule does NOT apply to nosological naming frames under Section 1.8 (identity alias, sole defining phenotype, or recognized clinical condition). Those spans are **Specific Disease** even when the head is a deficiency or state noun.
0230	- This rule does NOT block a patient-condition frame under Section 1.9 or Moderation Principle 5 (Section 1.5). Such a frame is not a descriptive finding under case (b).
0231	- This rule does NOT apply to eponym- or abbreviation-anchored names with a non-disease head that Section 1.10 labels (e.g., "Coats telangiectasis"). Their meaning comes from the eponym or the document-level expansion, not from a descriptive finding.
0232	- It does not remove labels assigned under other definitions. A span that independently meets the Disease Class, Composite Mention, or Moderation Principle 8 definition keeps that label. The rule only blocks **Specific Disease**.
0233	- Anaphoric spans are not re-labelled **Disease Class** either. They are umbrella placeholders ("What NOT to Annotate," item 4).
0234	- The rule is stated in terms of grammatical role and reference status, not in terms of any particular disease word, so it applies to unseen spans with the same structures.
0235	
0236	#### 1.8 Nosological-state spans that name the disorder
0237	A span whose head is a **nosological-state noun** (a noun denoting a deficiency, a deficient state, or a lesion or morphological state of the patient, such as "deficiency," "deficient activity," or "telangiectasis") is annotated as **Specific Disease**, together with its prepositional complement or gloss, when the span is the name or definition of one disorder under any of the following:
0238	
0239	- **(a) Identity alias, gloss, or appositive.** The span is introduced by a parenthetical, an appositive, or an alias marker ("so-called," "also known as," "also termed") and refers to the same condition as the named disorder. **Identity test:** substituting the named disorder for the span leaves the reference of the sentence unchanged. Alias markers are not part of the annotated span (Section 5).
0240	- **(b) Sole defining phenotype.** The span is the only defining phenotype in a "[disease] is caused by / characterized by [state]" frame and is not one item of a coordinated list. Only nosological-state heads qualify under this route. Other manifestations in the same frame remain **Disease Class** under Section 1.3.
0241	- **(c) Recognized clinical condition.** The state noun combines with a prepositional complement naming a specific enzyme, gene, or variant, and the whole string is the recognized name of a clinical condition. It is not a description of an enzyme's state in a sample, a mechanism, or an experiment. Compact fixed compounds (Section 1.2) are a special case of this route. In a patient-condition frame, Moderation Principle 5 (Section 1.5) supplies the same label for a privative head with a biomolecule complement.
0242	
0243	**Does not apply when:**
0244	- The state is a downstream mechanism or effect introduced by a result or consequence construction ("results in," "leads to," "secondary to," "responsible for"). Such spans are governed by Section 1.5 and are not annotated, except for a separately lexicalized disorder name inside them. Where the finding noun is the condition's own label by identity, Section 1.10(b) applies instead.
0245	- The state is a measured or biochemical quantity or activity of a gene product (e.g., "deficient activity of FALDH," "enzyme yield," "specific activity"), including when it appears in a characterizing list ("What NOT to Annotate," item 7; Rule Precedence, item 13).
0246	- The state is one item in a coordinated list of findings and has no alias, appositive, or identity relation to a named disease. Such items are governed by Section 1.3.
0247	- The state is an adjectival premodifier of a phenotype, patient, or sample (Section 3.2 → **Modifier**).
0248	- The state is a deficit-type head with a molecular complement introduced by "of" or "in." The span rule of Section 1.5 governs, and route (b) cannot promote the phrase. Routes (a) and (c) remain available (Rule Precedence, item 15), and Moderation Principle 5 (Section 1.5) provides the patient-condition label where its frame is present.
0249	- The span names a generic category (**Disease Class**; Sections 1.1 and 1.6) or coordinates several disorders (**Composite Mention**).
0250	- The parenthetical adds a manifestation or pathology of the disorder and fails the identity test. Such a gloss is **Disease Class** under Section 1.3 (e.g., "(exudative retinal detachment)," Example 32).
0251	- The span is a running-text reuse of an alternative name for an already-named disorder, with no alias construction. Such reuse is not separately annotated ("What NOT to Annotate," item 3).
0252	- The span is a biological-process nominal (e.g., "development," "accumulation"). Item 5 governs.
0253	
0254	**Interactions:** Section 1.5 (mechanism frames, the span rule for deficit constructions, and Moderation Principle 5) is applied before this section. This section is applied before Section 1.6, so a bare category head used in an alias construction is **Specific Disease** rather than **Disease Class**. Section 1.9 is applied after this section and only for patient-condition frames. Section 1.10 is applied after Section 1.9 and handles identity-labelled eponym and abbreviation names. Within this section, the identity test in (a) is applied before the feature-gloss test of Section 1.3.
0255	
0256	- **Example (alias):** "Coats disease is characterized by abnormal retinal vascular development (so-called retinal telangiectasis), which results in …" → "retinal telangiectasis" as **Specific Disease** (identity alias; "so-called" excluded from the span). Example 32.
0257	- **Example (recognized condition):** "Patients with deficiency of adenosine deaminase presented with recurrent infections." → "deficiency of adenosine deaminase" as **Specific Disease** (route (c)). Example 20.
0258	- **Non-example (mechanism):** "…which results in a deficiency of norrin…" → not annotated (result construction). Example 30.
0259	
0260	#### 1.9 Finding- or state-anchored named-target mentions
0261	IF a noun phrase's head is a finding noun (a clinical sign) or a state noun (a deficiency, absence, or deficient-activity construction), AND a specific named target anchors it, either as a proper-name eponym premodifier or as a named gene, enzyme, protein, or component in a prepositional complement or premodifier, AND the context presents that pairing as an inherited or clinical condition of a patient, THEN annotate the full noun phrase, including the anchoring eponym or complement, as **Specific Disease** rather than leaving it unannotated.
0262	
0263	**Relation to Section 1.5 (narrower rule first).** For a deficit-type head with a molecular complement, this section adds a Specific Disease label only where the whole string is a recognized clinical condition under Section 1.8(c) or a patient-condition frame under Moderation Principle 5 (Section 1.5). Otherwise the span-boundary rule of Section 1.5 governs, and the phrase is not tagged. Eponym-premodified finding nouns are not subject to this restriction (Rule Precedence, items 15 and 16).
0264	
0265	**Relation to Section 1.10.** Section 1.9 is applied first. Where it does not apply, or where its result-frame exclusion would remove a finding noun that is the condition's own label, Section 1.10 governs (Rule Precedence, item 19).
0266	
0267	**Does NOT apply when:**
0268	- (a) the target slot is unnamed or generic ("an enzyme," "some factor"), so no specific entity is named;
0269	- (b) the finding noun appears alone with no named anchor, such as a sign in a symptom list (Sections 1.3 and 1.4 govern);
0270	- (c) the construction reports an assay result, specimen property, or experimental manipulation rather than a condition of an organism. This includes a measured "deficient activity of [enzyme]" or "lacked … activity" construction inside a characterizing list (Section 1.7(b); "What NOT to Annotate," item 7; Rule Precedence, item 13);
0271	- (d) the head is already a lexicalized disease head ("…disease," "…syndrome," "…disorder," or a named-syndrome acronym), since the head itself supplies the disease label and the standard rules govern;
0272	- (e) the phrase coordinates multiple targets, in which case the Composite Mention boundary governs (Section 2.1);
0273	- (f) the construction is a mechanism or result frame ("results in," "leads to," "suppresses … resulting in," "secondary to," "responsible for"), because a mechanism is not a condition of the patient (Section 1.5), unless Section 1.10(b) applies because the finding noun names the eponymous condition by identity.
0274	
0275	- **Example (illustrative):** "The proband presented with a Lhermitte sign." → "Lhermitte sign" as **Specific Disease** (eponym premodifier on a finding noun, presented as the patient's clinical condition; Example 40).
0276	- **Non-example (mechanism):** "Hypermethylation suppressed transcription, resulting in absence of the FMR1 protein." → not annotated (frame (f); Section 1.5; Example 40).
0277	- **Non-example (assay):** "…deficient activity of fatty aldehyde dehydrogenase (FALDH)…" within the Sjögren-Larsson characterizing list → not annotated (frame (c); Example 31).
0278	- **Non-example (specimen):** "Her fresh serum lacked complement-mediated bactericidal activity…" → not annotated (frame (c); Example 4).
0279	
0280	#### 1.10 Named conditions without a nosological head (Moderation Principle 1)
0281	IF a candidate span names a specific condition through an eponym, a proper name, or an abbreviation, AND its head noun is not a disease-class noun (for example, a sign or finding noun such as "telangiectasis" or "sign," or no head at all), AND no nosological head or adjacent parenthetical expansion already anchors it, THEN do not leave it unannotated by default. Annotate the full noun phrase, including any prepositional complement, as **Specific Disease** when one of the following holds:
0282	
0283	- **(a) Identity under a causal or defining predicate.** The span is the complement of a causal or defining predicate (e.g., "identified as," "defined as," "classified as," "diagnosed as") that presents it as the identity of a condition named in the same sentence.
0284	- **(b) Finding noun as alternate label.** The span is a sign or finding noun used as an alternate label for an eponymous condition already named in the document. The eponym and the finding noun together form one span (e.g., "Coats telangiectasis").
0285	- **(c) Bare abbreviation with a document-level expansion.** The span is a bare abbreviation whose expansion, stated elsewhere in the document (title, abstract, or an earlier sentence), is a disease name, and the abbreviation is the argument of a condition frame (subject, object, or prepositional complement). An abbreviation defined by an adjacent parenthetical is handled by Section 6.
0286	
0287	**Does not apply when:**
0288	- the phrase is a gene-level, enzyme-level, or laboratory observation not presented as defining a named disorder (e.g., "FALDH," "deficient activity of FALDH," a mutation effect, a measured activity, or the cause of a different entity);
0289	- the head is a broad category term (e.g., a generic "cancer" or "disorder"), which remains **Disease Class** under Sections 1.1 and 1.6;
0290	- the phrase contains only a Modifier (an inheritance pattern, severity, or age descriptor) with no disease name;
0291	- the abbreviation's expansion refers to a gene, protein, assay, or drug rather than a disease (e.g., "NDP," "FMR1," "WASP");
0292	- the span is a premodifier of a gene or protein head (e.g., "ancient SLS genes"), which is **Modifier** under Section 3.1 (Rule Precedence, item 2);
0293	- the span is a deficit with a molecular complement, in which case the span rule of Section 1.5 governs unless Section 1.8(c) or Moderation Principle 5 applies;
0294	- the finding noun is a downstream mechanism or effect introduced by a result or consequence construction and does not name the condition by identity (Sections 1.5 and 1.9(f)). The identity relations in (a) and (b) are tested first;
0295	- the span is anaphoric (Section 1.7).
0296	
0297	**Relation to other sections.** Section 1.9 is applied first. Where Section 1.9 governs a patient-condition frame, its label stands. Section 1.10(b) supplies the identity route for finding nouns that Section 1.9(f) would exclude as result-frame mechanisms, because a finding noun that is the condition's own label is not a mechanism. Section 1.10 is applied before the named-entity test of Section 1.6, so an eponym-anchored span is not reduced to **Disease Class** there.
0298	
0299	- **Example (identity, illustrative):** "In this family, the disorder called Coats disease was identified as Coats telangiectasis." → "Coats telangiectasis" as **Specific Disease** (route (a); the complement of "identified as" names the condition named in the same sentence).
0300	- **Example (finding-noun label):** Example 44.
0301	- **Example (abbreviation):** Example 45.
0302	
0303	### 2. Annotate **contiguous text strings**
0304	A textual string may refer to two or more separate disease mentions. Such mentions are annotated with the **Composite Mention** category.
0305	
0306	**Example**  
0307	> The text phrase "Duchenne and Becker muscular dystrophy" refers to two separate diseases. If this phrase is separated into two strings: "Duchenne" and "Becker muscular dystrophy," it results in information loss, because the word "Duchenne" on its own is not a disease mention.
0308	
0309	#### 2.1 Shared-head coordination
0310	IF a coordinating conjunction joins two or more pre-head modifiers that together precede one unrepeated head noun within a single contiguous noun phrase, AND at least one modifier specifies an anatomical site, disease type, or pathological attribute, THEN label the full string from the first modifier through the shared head as one **Composite Mention**. Do not split the string at the conjunction, and do not label the leading modifier separately. The head noun does not need to appear in the disease lexicon. Nested **Modifier** labels that the guidelines require may remain inside the composite span.
0311	
0312	The rule is defined by syntax (coordinator, pre-head modifiers, shared head, noun phrase boundary) rather than by specific words. It applies to unseen anatomical and pathological modifiers and to pathological-state or finding heads.
0313	
0314	**Do NOT apply this rule** when the conjunction links:
0315	- (a) two complete noun phrases that each have their own head;
0316	- (b) clauses, predicates, or verb phrases;
0317	- (c) a second conjunct that repeats or introduces a different head noun;
0318	- (d) modifiers that are purely temporal, quantitative, procedural, or administrative rather than disease-specifying (e.g., "10 deletions, 2 insertions"), or a geographic specification such as "European or Middle Eastern descent," which specifies neither a disease type, an anatomical site, nor a pathological attribute;
0319	- (e) segments separated by punctuation, a parenthetical, or a prepositional phrase that attaches to a different head. Punctuation-separated disease lists remain under the list-based Composite rule in Section 2 (Example 7);
0320	- (f) a shared head that is a biological-process nominal ("What NOT to Annotate," item 5), even when the modifiers are anatomical (e.g., "intraretinal and subretinal lipid accumulation," Example 35). Such a span is not Composite. A deviation-qualified process or outcome in a characterizing or causal pathological frame is labelled as a single **Disease Class** span under Section 1.3 (Moderation Principle 2; Example 27). Moderation Principle 7 (below) does not extend to process heads.
0321	
0322	- **Examples:** "fibroepithelial or epithelial hyperplasias" (Example 18); "stage II or stage III colorectal cancer" (Example 19); "breast or ovarian cancer" (Example 34). Each is one **Composite Mention**.
0323	
0324	**Coordinated modifiers over a shared finding or event head (Moderation Principle 7).** IF a coordinator links two or more attributive modifiers (location or laterality terms, such as *intraretinal* and *subretinal*) whose meaning distributes over one shared, possibly elided head noun, AND that head is a finding or event noun (e.g., *hemorrhages*, *fluid*, *lesions*) rather than a disease-category noun, THEN label the full string from the first modifier through the shared head as one **Composite Mention**, because it denotes multiple distinct referents. This applies even when the surrounding context makes the phrase sound pathological: the coordinated shared-head structure is the trigger, and context alone is not.
0325	- Biological-process nominal heads (e.g., *accumulation*, *development*) are excluded. They remain governed by Section 2.1(f) and "What NOT to Annotate," item 5, and Moderation Principle 7 is applied to finding and event heads only (Rule Precedence item 24).
0326	- Where the shared head is a disease-category noun (e.g., *cancer*, *syndrome*, *detachment*), the coordinated modifiers are handled under the Modifier and Specific Disease / Disease Class rules.
0327	- A single conjunct with one modifier and one disease-category head remains **Disease Class**. A coordinator joining two complete noun phrases that each have their own head is not this rule. A geographic specification (Section 2.1(d)) is not this rule.
0328	- A modifier used attributively inside a larger phrase with no coordination remains **Modifier**.
0329	
0330	- **Example:** "Fundus examination showed intraretinal and subretinal hemorrhages." → "intraretinal and subretinal hemorrhages" as **Composite Mention** (Example 51).
0331	
0332	### 3. Annotate disease mentions that are used as **Modifiers** for other concepts
0333	A textual string may refer to a disease name, but it may modify a noun phrase (or may not be a noun phrase itself). In such cases, annotate using the **Modifier** category. Modifier labelling is determined before the Specific Disease and Disease Class tests in Sections 1.1 through 1.10 and Section 3.3 are applied to the same span (see Rule Precedence). Sections 3.1, 3.2, and the attributive role of Section 3.4(b) decide Modifier status; the complement and argument roles of Sections 3.4(a) and 3.4(b) decide the non-Modifier labels.
0334	
0335	**Example**  
0336	> Although this mutation was initially detected in four of 33 colorectal cancer families analysed from eastern England, more extensive analysis has reduced the frequency to four of 52 English HNPCC kindreds analysed.
0337	
0338	- **Annotate**:  
0339	  - "colorectal cancer" as **Modifier**  
0340	  - "HNPCC" as **Modifier**
0341	
0342	#### 3.1 Disease premodifiers of gene or protein heads
0343	If a disease name, disease acronym, or eponym is the leftmost element of a noun phrase whose head noun denotes a gene, protein, locus, or similar molecular unit (with or without a numeric or alphanumeric suffix), annotate only the disease tokens as **Modifier**. The head noun, any numeric or alphanumeric suffix, and any bracketed gene symbol are left unannotated. The compound's resemblance to a gene or protein name does not change this label. This rule takes precedence over the determiner trigger in Section 3.3.
0344	
0345	- **Examples**  
0346	  - "ancient SLS genes" → "SLS" as **Modifier**  
0347	  - "breast cancer susceptibility gene 1 (BRCA1)" → "breast cancer" as **Modifier**; "gene 1 (BRCA1)" unannotated  
0348	  - "the DTD gene" → "DTD" as **Modifier**  
0349	  - "the Wiskott-Aldrich syndrome protein (WASP)" → "Wiskott-Aldrich syndrome" as **Modifier**; "protein (WASP)" unannotated
0350	
0351	**Do not apply this rule** when the disease term is not a premodifier of a gene or protein head:
0352	- a standalone disease name with no following molecular head (e.g., "Coats disease");
0353	- a disease term that heads the phrase or modifies a clinical or epidemiological head (e.g., "ovarian cancer cases," "ovarian cancer population"). Existing Modifier rules govern these (Section 3.3, negative constraint; Section 3.4(b));
0354	- a disease term that is the complement of a preposition, unless Section 3.4 applies;
0355	- spans in which the leftmost element is a gene or enzyme symbol rather than a disease name (e.g., "G6PD A- genes"), or a gene name built from non-disease words.
0356	
0357	#### 3.2 Privative adjectives modifying a phenotype, patient, or sample
0358	When a privative adjective modifies a phenotype, patient, or sample, the disease-containing span is a **Modifier** and is not a Specific Disease. This is the negative case of Section 1.2.
0359	
0360	- **Example:** "G6PD deficient" in "the G6PD deficient phenotype" → **Modifier**.
0361	
0362	Moderation Principle 5 (Section 1.5) governs only a privative noun that heads the phrase. A privative adjective is not labelled under it, and remains a **Modifier** when it modifies a phenotype, patient, or sample.
0363	
0364	#### 3.3 Referent introduction versus attributive label
0365	A disease name is annotated as **Specific Disease**, not **Modifier**, when it is introduced as a referent. A disease name is introduced as a referent when it:
0366	- (i) takes an introducing determiner (such as "a" or "the");
0367	- (ii) serves as a clause-level subject or object;
0368	- (iii) serves as the prepositional complement naming the condition that a person, group, or sample has or is characterized by (e.g., "probands … with SLS," "a unilateral variant of Coats disease," "affected by Norrie disease," "the classical form of Coats disease"); or
0369	- (iv) is followed by a parenthetical abbreviation that defines it.
0370	
0371	Post-nominal participial or other modifying material does not change the label. A parenthetical abbreviation that defines a **Specific Disease** head inherits **Specific Disease** from its antecedent. It must not inherit the label of a larger span that contains it. A parenthetical that follows a non-disease head (e.g., "Wiskott-Aldrich syndrome protein (WASP)") inherits nothing.
0372	
0373	**Negative constraint (Modifier).** Do not apply this rule when the disease string is a non-referential attributive label fused into a noun phrase whose head denotes a non-disease entity: a population, case set, cohort, patient group, study, family-history record, risk, gene, protein, locus, or phenotype. In that case the string is **Modifier**. This covers a premodifier of such a head (e.g., "the ovarian cancer population," "ancient SLS genes," "the Wiskott-Aldrich syndrome protein"). A disease string that is the object of a preposition governed by such a head (e.g., "a family history of ovarian cancer") is not a Modifier. It is governed by Section 3.4(a).
0374	
0375	A coordinated string naming two or more disorders is a **Composite Mention** under Section 2.1, not a Modifier (e.g., "a family history of breast or ovarian cancer").
0376	
0377	This rule also does not cover generic common-noun uses of "disease" that do not name a specific condition, or cases where the disease name is hyphen-fused into a compound premodifier of a non-disease head. Running-text co-referential alternative names are governed by "What NOT to Annotate," item 3, and are not labelled here.
0378	
0379	- **Examples:**  
0380	  - "Sjogren-Larsson syndrome (SLS) is an autosomal recessive disorder…" → "Sjogren-Larsson syndrome" and "SLS" as **Specific Disease** (clause-level subject; parenthetical abbreviation).  
0381	  - "…probands from 63 kindreds with SLS." → "SLS" as **Specific Disease** (prepositional complement naming the patients' condition).  
0382	  - "…in the ovarian cancer population…" → "ovarian cancer" as **Modifier** (attributive label on a population head).  
0383	  - "…a family history of ovarian cancer…" → "ovarian cancer" as **Specific Disease** (prepositional complement; Section 3.4(a)).
0384	
0385	#### 3.4 Disease mentions in prepositional complements and syntactic role (Moderation Principles 3 and 4)
0386	
0387	**(a) Prepositional complements are not Modifiers (Moderation Principle 3).** A disease mention is not labelled **Modifier** when it is the object of a preposition attached to a governing noun, such as a noun denoting history, risk, form, or the status of relatives or patients (e.g., "family history of …," "risk of …," "probands with …"). Such a mention is labelled:
0388	- **Specific Disease** when it names a particular disease entity, including a fixed site-plus-category compound such as "ovarian cancer" (Section 1.6(iii)) or an abbreviation (e.g., "SLS" in "probands … with SLS");
0389	- **Disease Class** when it is a generic or unspecified category term with no named type (e.g., bare "cancer" in "a family history of cancer").
0390	
0391	**Modifier** is reserved for an attributive premodifier immediately preceding a non-disease head: a population, study, series, case set, cohort, patient group, risk, gene, protein, locus, or phenotype (e.g., "the ovarian cancer population," "ovarian cancer risk," "ancient SLS genes"). The test is whether the disease term is itself the complement of the preposition. A disease term that premodifies the head of a prepositional object is governed by the premodifier rule instead. For example, in "activation of Wiskott-Aldrich syndrome protein," the disease term premodifies "protein," so Section 3.1 applies.
0392	
0393	**(b) Syntactic role decides Modifier status (Moderation Principle 4).**
0394	- **Argument role.** IF a disease mention is the head of its own noun phrase, the complement of a preposition or verb, or a clausal subject, THEN it is not a Modifier. It is **Specific Disease** when it names a particular condition, and **Disease Class** when it is a generic category term (Section 1.6). This holds even when the sentence is framed around risk, association, family history, or epidemiology, and even when the identical string is labelled Modifier elsewhere in the document.
0395	- **Attributive role.** IF a disease mention is an unmarked prenominal noun-adjunct, not governed by a preposition, that directly precedes a head noun it characterizes (population, case set, patient group, study, series, risk, gene, or protein), THEN it is a **Modifier**.
0396	- **Decision order.** Apply the syntactic-role test first. Use lexical or topical cues only after the role is established.
0397	
0398	**(c) Negative constraints.** These rules do not apply when (i) the disease string is part of a larger span governed by the Disease Class or Composite Mention definitions, since those take precedence (Rule Precedence, items 1 and 21); (ii) the disease string is part of a single lexicalized disease name that takes its entity type as a unit (e.g., "deficiency of adenosine deaminase" as a recognized condition, Section 1.8(c)); or (iii) the syntactic role is genuinely ambiguous, such as a noun–noun compound that could be either head or adjunct. In case (iii), defer to the entity definitions (Sections 1.1, 1.6, and 3.1), not to how often the string is labelled Modifier in the document. Coordinated disorders remain Composite (Section 2.1). For example, "a family history of breast or ovarian cancer" is one Composite Mention.
0399	
0400	**Examples:** Examples 37, 46, 48.
0401	
0402	#### 3.5 Note on exclusions
0403	Modifier labels are never converted into Specific Disease by Section 1.8, Section 1.9, or Section 1.10 when the span is a premodifier of a gene or protein head (Section 3.1) or a premodifier of a non-disease head under Section 3.4(b).
0404	
0405	### 4. Annotate **duplicate mentions**
0406	For each sentence in the PubMed abstract and title, the locations of all disease mentions are marked, including duplicates within the same sentence.
0407	
0408	### 5. Annotate **minimum necessary span of text**
0409	Use the smallest span necessary to include all tokens expressing the most specific form of the disease.
0410	
0411	**Example**  
0412	> In the case of "insulin-dependent diabetes mellitus," the disease mention including the whole phrase was preferred over its substrings such as "diabetes mellitus" or "diabetes."
0413	
0414	For privative fixed-nomenclature compounds under Section 1.2, the span is the whole compound, including the gene-product name, because splitting it loses the condition name. For free "of" or "in" complements, a Specific Disease span never extends across the preposition into the molecular entity unless Section 1.8(c) applies, in which case the complement is part of the recognized condition name. Under Moderation Principle 5 (Section 1.5), a patient-condition privative phrase is a Specific Disease span from the privative head through the final token of the biomolecule name; pre-head intensifiers and pre-head nouns, and parenthetical acronyms or abbreviations of the biomolecule, are excluded. For deficit-type constructions with molecular complements outside Moderation Principle 5, the span ends before the preposition, and the severity, zygosity, laterality, or organ modifiers attached to the deficit phrase are excluded with it (Section 1.5 span rule). For gene premodifiers under Section 3.1, only the disease tokens are the span. Alias markers ("so-called," "also known as") are not part of the disease span.
0415	
0416	### 6. Annotate all **synonymous mentions**
0417	Abbreviation definitions such as "Huntington disease" ("HD") are separated into two annotated mentions (one for the long form, one for the abbreviation). The same applies to a descriptive alias introduced by a parenthetical or alias marker that passes the identity test of Section 1.8(a) (e.g., "unilateral retinal telangiectasis" in a title parenthetical). Running-text reuses of an alias are not separately annotated ("What NOT to Annotate," item 3). An abbreviation whose expansion appears elsewhere in the document, rather than in an adjacent parenthetical, is handled by Section 1.10(c).

0421	## Rule Precedence for Overlapping Principles
0422	
0423	The principles above were derived independently and may overlap. The narrower condition is applied first, and each broader rule explicitly excludes the narrower cases. Labels "Principle 1" through "Principle 4" in Sections 1.1, 1.3, 1.5, and 1.9 refer to principles integrated in an earlier revision. "Moderation Principle 1" through "Moderation Principle 4" refer to principles integrated in an earlier moderation revision: Moderation Principle 1 is in Section 1.10, Moderation Principle 2 is in the compositional form of Section 1.3, and Moderation Principles 3 and 4 are in Section 3.4. The principles integrated in the current revision are numbered Moderation Principles 5 to 8: Moderation Principle 5 (privative patient-condition spans, Section 1.5), Moderation Principle 6 (generic condition nouns in patient-presentation frames, Section 1.4), Moderation Principle 7 (coordinated modifiers over a shared finding or event head, Section 2.1), and Moderation Principle 8 (causal condition-state Disease Class, Section 1.3). Items 22 to 25 below state where these are applied in the sequence. Where two rules would label the same span differently, apply them in this order:
0424	
0425	1. **Composite Mention** (Section 2 and Section 2.1, including Moderation Principle 7) overrides Sections 1.1 through 1.10 and Sections 3.3 and 3.4. A coordinated string naming two or more disorders is Composite, including inside population or family-history frames (e.g., "a family history of breast or ovarian cancer"). Composite spans follow their own definitions and are not treated as Section 1.4 feature glosses. Section 1.9(e) defers to this item.
0426	2. **Modifier labelling** (Sections 3.1 and 3.2, and the attributive role of Section 3.4(b)) is determined before the Specific Disease and Disease Class tests are applied to the same span. A span labelled Modifier is excluded from Sections 1.1 through 1.10 and Section 3.3. Section 1.4 does not govern Modifier boundaries.
0427	3. **Referent introduction** (Section 3.3) is applied only to spans not labelled Modifier under item 2. Its non-referential negative constraint returns population, case-set, patient-group, gene, and protein premodifiers to Modifier. It does not return prepositional complements to Modifier; those are governed by item 21.
0428	4. **Name-versus-reference gate** (Section 1.7) is applied before any Specific Disease assignment. It blocks Specific Disease for anaphoric and descriptive-finding spans, but not for nosological naming frames under Section 1.8, patient-condition frames under Section 1.9 or Moderation Principle 5, or eponym- and abbreviation-anchored names under Section 1.10 (item 19). It does not remove a Disease Class or Composite label established under another rule.
0429	5. **Mechanism and measured-finding frames** (Section 1.5, "What NOT to Annotate," item 7, and Section 1.9(c) and (f)) are applied before Section 1.8. A deficiency introduced by a result or consequence construction, or a measured gene-product quantity, cannot be promoted to Specific Disease by Section 1.8 or Section 1.10. These frames also block the compositional Disease Class of item 20, and the causal condition-state Disease Class of item 25 except for clinical pathogenic outcomes.
0430	6. **Nosological naming** (Section 1.8) is applied before the feature-gloss test of Section 1.3 and before the named-entity test of Section 1.6. Within Section 1.8, the identity test in (a) is applied before the gloss test: a parenthetical that refers to the same disorder is **Specific Disease**, and one that adds a manifestation is **Disease Class**. Route (b) (sole defining phenotype) overrides Section 1.3 for nosological-state heads only, and does not apply to deficit heads with molecular complements (item 15). Items in a coordinated list remain under Section 1.3.
0431	7. **Post-nominal molecular complements** (Section 1.5) are applied before Section 1.6 and before the fixed-compound rule (Section 1.2). Section 1.5 excludes compact fixed compounds (which go to Section 1.2 as Specific Disease) and adjectival forms (which are Modifier). The patient-condition paragraph (item 22) is an exception to the span boundary for its own frame only.
0432	8. **Fixed-nomenclature compounds** (Section 1.2) assign Specific Disease to the whole string. Section 1.6 explicitly preserves these compounds.
0433	9. **Named-entity versus category** (Section 1.6) applies after items 4 through 8, items 16, 18, 19, and 22. Its free "X of Y" Disease Class trigger applies only where the complement does not form a recognized condition under Section 1.8(c) or a patient-condition frame under item 22. Its bare-category trigger also governs generic terms in prepositional complements (item 21).
0434	10. **Referent over form** (Section 1.4) is applied before the generic-descriptor trigger in Section 1.3. Its case-level and alias Specific Disease triggers override the feature-position default, including the patient-presentation rule of Moderation Principle 6, which applies to generic condition nouns presented as an individual patient's condition (item 23). Its feature-gloss and "characterized by" Disease Class triggers agree with Section 1.3, including the manifestation-frame treatment of bare sign nouns.
0435	11. **Individuated generic heads** (Section 1.1) override the bare-term exclusion in "What NOT to Annotate," item 4, only when the mention is individuated. Umbrella, enumerated, and anaphoric uses remain excluded.
0436	12. **Biological-process nominals** ("What NOT to Annotate," item 5) override the pathological-process trigger in Section 1.3, the manifestation-frame treatment in Section 1.3, the deviation-operator paragraph of Section 1.3 (item 17), and the shared-head rule in Section 2.1(f). The exception is a deviation-qualified process nominal in a characterizing or causal pathological-outcome frame, which is labelled under item 20. Moderation Principle 7 (item 24) does not extend to process heads.
0437	13. **Measured or mechanistic quantities** ("What NOT to Annotate," item 7) override the Section 1.3 trigger for gene-product activity, even when the activity appears in a "characterized by" list. They also override Section 1.8(b), Section 1.9(c), Moderation Principle 2(c) (item 20), and the patient-condition frame of item 22 for measured activity.
0438	14. **Named disorders** are governed by Sections 1 and 1.1, by Section 1.4 for lowercase common-noun forms, and by Section 1.8 for alias and naming forms. They are not governed by Section 1.3. Eponym- or site-named conditions are **Specific Disease**. Eponym-anchored finding nouns that name a condition by identity are governed by Section 1.10 (item 19).
0439	15. **Deficit-construction span boundary** (Section 1.5, Principle 2) is the narrower condition for a deficit-type head with an "of" or "in" molecular complement. It is applied together with item 5, before items 6 and 16. It blocks Specific Disease on the deficit phrase and its molecular complement, so Section 1.8(b) and Section 1.9 cannot promote it. Its exemptions (lexicalized condition names, Section 1.8(c) recognized conditions, Section 1.8(a) identity aliases, the patient-condition frame of item 22, disorder-naming heads, and non-molecular complements) return the span to the Specific Disease rules. This item does not alter Disease Class decisions, including the compositional form of item 20 and the causal condition-state form of item 25.
0440	16. **Finding- or state-anchored named-target mentions** (Section 1.9, Principle 1) is the broader condition. It is applied after items 4, 5, 13, and 15. It is excluded for assay, measured, mechanism, result-frame, and lexicalized-head cases, and for deficit-with-molecular-complement cases unless item 15's exemption or item 22 applies. A result-frame exclusion does not apply where the finding noun is the condition's own label (item 19). It does not override Composite Mention (item 1).
0441	17. **Deviation-operator Disease Class** (Section 1.3, deviation paragraph, Principle 3) is applied after items 4, 9, 14, and 16, so that Specific Disease assignments, lexicalized names, and recognized conditions are not relabelled. It is excluded for biological-process heads except as item 20 provides (item 12), for molecular complements of deficit nominals except where item 20 or item 25 applies to a clinical characterizing or pathogenic feature (items 5, 7, and 15), for measured values (item 13), and for causal mutation, variant, or agent frames ("What NOT to Annotate," item 8). Item 20 is the compositional extension of this item, and item 25 extends it to gene-product deficit heads.
0442	18. **Etiology-anchored condition mentions** (Section 1.1, etiology paragraph, Principle 4) are Specific Disease assignments. They are applied before the bare-head and "X of Y" defaults of Section 1.6, and they yield to the manifestation-list convention (item 10) and the umbrella exclusions (item 11). Inheritance-pattern qualifiers do not trigger them.
0443	19. **Named conditions without a nosological head** (Section 1.10, Moderation Principle 1) are applied after items 1 through 3, 13, 15, and 16, and before item 9. Where the item-16 frame governs, its label stands. This item applies Specific Disease to eponym-, finding-noun-, and abbreviation-anchored names with non-disease heads. It yields to item 2 (Modifier), to item 5 (measured and mechanistic frames), and to the exclusions of Section 1.10 (gene-level, enzyme-level, laboratory, broad-category, and Modifier-only spans).
0444	20. **Compositional pathological-state Disease Class** (Section 1.3, Moderation Principle 2) is applied after items 4, 13, and 15. It is the compositional extension of item 17. It takes precedence over the process-nominal exclusion of item 12 for deviation-qualified process heads in characterizing or causal outcome frames. It yields: (a) to Section 1.5 and item 5 for molecular complements in a mechanism or result-introduced frame, which is the narrower gene-product rule and is not reopened by the "results in" trigger (clinical pathogenic outcomes of such complements are governed by item 25); (b) to item 13 for measured values, experimental manipulations, and reagent states; (c) to item 1 for coordinated multi-condition mentions; (d) to item 8 for causal agents; and (e) to item 4 and Section 1.4 for lexicalized names. Normal or non-deviation process nominals remain excluded.
0445	21. **Prepositional-complement and syntactic-role test** (Section 3.4; Moderation Principles 3 and 4) is applied after items 1 and 2. It decides Modifier versus non-Modifier status and, within non-Modifier mentions, between Specific Disease and Disease Class. Where a generic category term sits in a prepositional complement, the narrower bare-category rule of Section 1.6(a) and Moderation Principle 3 yields Disease Class. It governs before the broader syntactic-role default of Moderation Principle 4, which would otherwise yield Specific Disease. Gene- and protein-premodifier Modifier labels (Section 3.1) and Composite spans remain above this item. This item also governs bare broad category nouns for Moderation Principle 6 (item 23).
0446	22. **Privative patient-condition spans** (Section 1.5, Moderation Principle 5) are the narrower condition for a privative head with a biomolecule complement in a patient-condition frame. They are applied after items 5 and 13, so that mechanism, result-introduced, and measured frames stay unannotated, and before items 9, 15, and 16, which they override where the frame is present. They yield to item 1 (Composite), item 2 (Modifier, e.g., "G6PD deficient phenotype"), and item 8 (causal agents). Where the patient-condition frame is absent, item 15 and Section 1.2 govern as before.
0447	23. **Generic condition nouns in patient-presentation frames** (Section 1.4, Moderation Principle 6) are applied within item 10. A coordinated list of such nouns under a patient-presentation frame is labelled Specific Disease item by item; the same list under a type-level predicate is Disease Class (item 10 and Section 1.3). Coordinated modifiers that share one head are governed first by item 24. Bare broad category nouns in a history, risk, or population complement remain under item 21.
0448	24. **Coordinated modifiers over a shared finding or event head** (Section 2.1, Moderation Principle 7) is Composite, and item 1 governs it. It is applied before items 10 and 20 for finding and event heads, so that the Section 1.3 and Section 1.4 labels and the compositional Disease Class of item 20 are not applied to the same span. It does not apply to biological-process heads (item 12 and Section 2.1(f)), to disease-category heads, to single conjuncts with one disease-category head, or to geographic specifications.
0449	25. **Causal condition-state Disease Class** (Section 1.3, Moderation Principle 8) is applied after items 4, 13, 15, and 22. It extends item 17 to gene-product deficit heads. It yields to: item 22 for patient-condition and cause-of-disease frames; item 13 for measured quantities and activities; item 5 and Section 1.5 for molecular mechanism or hypothesis frames, which remain unannotated (the "results in" trigger does not reopen them); item 4 for umbrella heads; and item 8 for causal agents. Where items 22 and 25 would both apply, item 22 governs.
0450	
0451	**Relations among Moderation Principles 5 to 8.** Where these overlap, the narrower condition is applied first:
0452	- Moderation Principle 5 is applied before Moderation Principle 8. A privative biomolecule phrase framed as a patient's condition, a clinical presentation, or the cause of a named disease is Specific Disease and is not also Disease Class. Moderation Principle 8 applies only to clinical pathogenic outcomes outside those frames, and never to mechanism or hypothesis frames.
0453	- Moderation Principle 7 is applied before Moderation Principle 6. Coordinated location or laterality modifiers that share one finding or event head form one Composite Mention. Moderation Principle 6 governs coordinated items that each carry their own head.
0454	- Moderation Principle 6 does not govern deficit heads with molecular complements (those belong to Moderation Principles 5 and 8), and Moderation Principles 5 and 8 do not govern sign or symptom nouns.
0455	
0456	Where a rule states that it does not apply to a case handled by another rule, that exclusion is binding. The narrower rule is applied first.

0460	## What NOT to Annotate
0461	
0462	1. **Do not annotate organism names**  
0463	   - Organism names such as "human" were excluded from the preferred mention.  
0464	   - Viruses, bacteria, and other organism names were not annotated **unless** it was clear that the disease caused by these organisms is being discussed.  
0465	   - **Example**  
0466	     > Studies of biopsied tissue for the presence of Epstein-Barr virus and cytomegalovirus were negative.  
0467	     
0468	     In this case: "Epstein-Barr virus" and "cytomegalovirus" are annotated as **Specific Disease** category **only** if the context clearly indicates the diseases they cause, otherwise not.
0469	
0470	2. **Do not annotate gender**  
0471	   - Tokens such as "male" and "female" were only included if they specifically identified a new form of the disease.  
0472	   - **Example**  
0473	     > "male breast cancer"
0474	
0475	3. **Do not annotate overlapping mentions**  
0476	   - **Example**: The phrase "von Hippel-Lindau (VHL) disease" is annotated as **one single disease mention** (Specific Disease category).  
0477	   - Running-text alternative names that co-refer to the same condition are not annotated as separate mentions (Section 1.4).  
0478	   - Parenthetical, appositive, or alias-marked alternative names that pass the identity test are **Specific Disease** mentions under Section 1.8(a), and are annotated as separate mentions like abbreviations (Section 6). Parenthetical glosses that add a manifestation rather than naming the disorder are **Disease Class** (Section 1.3).
0479	
0480	4. **Do not annotate general terms**  
0481	   - Very general terms such as "disease," "syndrome," "deficiency," "complications," "abnormalities," etc. are excluded when used bare or as unspecified umbrella placeholders.  
0482	   - These terms are annotated when they refer to one individuated condition (Section 1.1), when they are part of a specific name (e.g., "C7 deficiency," Section 1.2), or when they are generic condition nouns presented as an individual patient's condition (Moderation Principle 6; Section 1.4).  
0483	   - Anaphoric or demonstrative placeholders that point back to a condition named elsewhere (e.g., "this condition," "such disorder") are not annotated (Section 1.7).  
0484	   - However, the terms "cancer" and "tumor" were retained. A bare, generic "cancer" in a prepositional complement (e.g., "a family history of cancer") is **Disease Class** (Section 3.4(a)).
0485	
0486	5. **Do not annotate references to biological processes**  
0487	   - Terms corresponding to biological processes such as "tumorigenesis" or "cancerogenesis" are not annotated.  
0488	   - Process nominals (e.g., "development," "accumulation") are not annotated when they are neutral or normal, including when they occur with no deviation marker, because they describe mechanisms rather than states of the patient (Section 1.3).  
0489	   - A process nominal preceded by a deviation marker and used as the argument of a characterizing or causal pathological-outcome predicate is **not** excluded. It is labelled **Disease Class** under the compositional form of Section 1.3 (Moderation Principle 2; Rule Precedence, items 12 and 20). For example, "abnormal retinal vascular development" after "is characterized by" (Example 27).  
0490	   - This exclusion also applies within coordinated anatomical modifiers that share a process head unless Moderation Principle 2 labels the full span (Section 2.1(f); Example 35).
0491	
0492	6. **Do not annotate disease mentions interrupted by nested mentions**  
0493	   - Essentially, do not break the contiguous text rule.  
0494	   - **Example**  
0495	     > WT1 dysfunction is implicated in both neoplastic (Wilms tumor, mesothelioma, leukemia, and breast cancer) and nonneoplastic (glomerulosclerosis) disease.  
0496	     
0497	     Here, the mentions "neoplastic disease" and "nonneoplastic disease" are not annotated because other tokens break up these phrases. A parenthetical between coordinated modifiers also blocks Section 2.1 (Section 2.1(e)).
0498	
0499	7. **Do not annotate mechanistic, biochemical, or experimental quantities**  
0500	   - Measured properties of a gene product or experimental system, such as specific activity, enzyme yield, thermal stability, expression level, or measured deficient activity (e.g., "deficient activity of FALDH"), are not annotated, even when they appear in a characterizing list (Sections 1.3 and 1.7; Section 1.8 exclusions; Moderation Principle 2(c); Moderation Principle 5 negative constraints).  
0501	   - Post-nominal molecular complements that describe a mechanism (e.g., "a deficiency of norrin within the developing retina") are likewise not annotated (Section 1.5). A clinical pathogenic outcome of a mutation, stated at the disease level, is **Disease Class** under Moderation Principle 8 (Section 1.3); this does not reopen mechanism or hypothesis frames.  
0502	   - The exception is a phrase that names a condition under Section 1.2 (fixed nomenclature compound), Section 1.8 (naming frame, including a recognized clinical condition under Section 1.8(c)), Section 1.9 (patient-condition frame that is not excluded by its own negative conditions), Section 1.10 (named condition by identity), or Moderation Principle 5 (a privative biomolecule phrase in a patient-condition frame).
0503	
0504	8. **Do not annotate causal etiological agents, even inside a deviation or pathological frame.**  
0505	   - Mutations, variants, alterations, polymorphisms, expansions, and exposures are not annotated as **Disease Class** merely because a deviation term or pathological outcome is attached to them (Section 1.3, deviation paragraph; Principle 3).  
0506	   - **Example:** "somatic mutation in the NDP gene" (Example 29) and "germline BRCA1 alterations" are not annotated.  
0507	   - A named disorder that is the outcome of such an agent is annotated under its own rules.
