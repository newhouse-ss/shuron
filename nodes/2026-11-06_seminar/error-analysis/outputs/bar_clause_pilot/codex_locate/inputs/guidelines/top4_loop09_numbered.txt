0001	# Biomedical Annotation Guidelines
0002	
0003	## What to Annotate
0004	
0005	### 1. Annotate all **Specific Disease** mentions
0006	A textual string referring to a disease name may refer to a **Specific Disease** or a **Disease Class**.  
0007	- **Disease Class**: Mentions that could be described as a family of multiple specific diseases.  
0008	- **Specific Disease**: Mentions that can be linked to one specific definition that does not include further categorization.
0009	
0010	**Example**  
0011	> Diastrophic dysplasia is an autosomal recessive disease characterized by short stature, very short limbs and joint problems that restrict mobility.
0012	
0013	- **Annotate**:  
0014	  - “Diastrophic dysplasia” as **Specific Disease**  
0015	  - “autosomal recessive disease” as **Disease Class**
0016	
0017	#### 1.1 Referential Scope: Specific Disease vs. Disease Class
0018	
0019	Decide the label from what a disease-denoting noun phrase refers to in context, not from the category-like meaning of its head noun.
0020	
0021	- **Label as Specific Disease** when the phrase is anchored to one individuated condition, either:
0022	  - by a proper name, eponym, acronym, gene, or pathogen identity (e.g., “Norrie disease,” “G6PD deficiency,” “C7 deficiency”), or
0023	  - by a patient-level clinical role, meaning it is the argument of a predicate that expresses a specific patient’s presentation, diagnosis, or finding (e.g., “presenting with,” “found to have,” “diagnosed with,” “affected by,” or a patient “with” a condition).  
0024	
0025	  This holds even when the head noun is a lexically generic umbrella term (e.g., “disease,” “disorder,” “cancer”) and/or the phrase carries a subtype or site modifier.
0026	  - “a son affected by Norrie disease” → “Norrie disease” = **Specific Disease**
0027	  - “a female with a unilateral variant of Coats disease” → “Coats disease” = **Specific Disease**
0028	
0029	- **Label as Disease Class** when:
0030	  - the phrase is a superordinate category reference that enumerates or classifies kinds of disease (introduced by “such as,” “including,” “types of,” or a class-membership frame, e.g., “is an autosomal recessive disease”); or
0031	  - the phrase is a phenotype or feature term listed in a generic, definitional description of a syndrome or entity, rather than a condition that a specific individual has or is diagnosed with (e.g., “mental retardation” in “…is characterized by ichthyosis, mental retardation, and spasticity”).
0032	
0033	- **Family-history and cohort uses are not patient-level roles.** A disease named in a family history (“a family history of ovarian cancer”) describes relatives, and a disease named as a cohort descriptor (“ovarian cancer cases”) describes a group. Both are **Modifier** (see Section 3). A coordinated list of two or more diseases in such a descriptor is a **Composite Mention** (Section 2).
0034	
0035	- **Modifiers do not change the category label.** A subtype, site, laterality, or severity modifier alone does not change the label in either direction. Span boundaries are governed by Section 5.
0036	
0037	- **Precedence.** The anchoring rule above applies only to disease-denoting heads. Attachment (Section 3.1), coordination (Section 2), referential alias (Section 1.3), deficit spans (Section 5.2), state and feature terms (Section 1.2), and the diagnostic-target rule (Section 1.4) override it where they apply, in the order given in “Principle Precedence.”
0038	
0039	#### 1.2 Pathological States, Signs, and Deficiencies Without a Fixed Disease Name
0040	
0041	- **Label as Disease Class** when a span denotes a pathological state, clinical sign, or deficiency without a fixed disease name, either because its head noun is a state, sign, abnormality, or deficiency noun, or because a deviation qualifier (such as “abnormal,” “deficient,” or “defective”) modifies a process or substance noun, **and** the span is linked to a named disorder through a characterization, causal, or consequence frame (e.g., “is characterized by,” “is caused by,” “results in,” “secondary to”). Tag the entire span, including the qualifier or state head. Do not tag an embedded process, molecule, or gene name within that span as a separate entity.
0042	  - “Coats disease is characterized by abnormal retinal vascular development” → “abnormal retinal vascular development” = **Disease Class**; “Coats disease” remains **Specific Disease**.
0043	  - “…which results in a deficiency of norrin within the developing retina” (a consequence of a somatic NDP mutation) → “deficiency of norrin” = **Disease Class**; “norrin” is not tagged separately. Section 5.2 does not extend this span because the deficit is not inborn or heritable.
0044	
0045	- **The span must specify what is abnormal.** Bare umbrella nouns that do not specify the abnormality (e.g., “complications,” “abnormalities,” “infectious complications”) are not annotated (see “What NOT to Annotate,” item 4). Specified states with no linked named disorder are not labeled under this section (see Example 18).
0046	
0047	- **Negative constraints.** Do NOT apply this section, and label the span according to the Specific Disease or Modifier definitions instead, when:
0048	  - the span is a conventional disorder name or an attributive disease-name use (e.g., “G6PD deficiency” and “C7 deficiency” are **Specific Disease**; “G6PD deficient” modifying “phenotype” is **Modifier**);
0049	  - the process, gene, or protein lacks a deviation qualifier or pathological-state head (e.g., “tumorigenesis,” “cancerogenesis”);
0050	  - the qualifier describes a healthy, normal, or therapeutic state;
0051	  - the head is a sequence-level entity, such as a genetic variant, mutation, alteration, or polymorphism (e.g., “germline BRCA1 alterations,” “missense mutations”). These are not pathological states in themselves, even when linked to a disorder;
0052	  - the state is measured in an experiment or assay (in vitro, expression system, or laboratory test) rather than being a condition of a patient or population (e.g., “a severe reduction of FALDH enzyme catalytic activity when expressed in mammalian cells”; “enzyme deficiency in G6PD A-” inferred from a bacterial expression study). See also “What NOT to Annotate,” item 7;
0053	  - the span is a finding- or state-headed alias of a named disease, introduced by a naming marker or alias gloss (Section 1.3).
0054	
0055	#### 1.3 Referential Alias for Finding- and State-Headed Names
0056	
0057	A noun phrase whose head is a clinical-finding or biochemical-state noun (e.g., “telangiectasis,” “deficiency,” “deficit”), and that is not a nosological head such as “disease,” “syndrome,” “disorder,” or “cancer,” is labeled **Specific Disease** when it names the disease under discussion rather than describing a manifestation. This holds when either of the following applies.
0058	
0059	- **(a) Alias.** The phrase co-refers with a disease named in the same or the immediately preceding sentence, and it is introduced by a metalinguistic naming marker (“so-called,” “also known as,” “termed,” “referred to as”) or appears as a parenthetical gloss that gives the disease’s alternative name.
0060	  - “Coats disease is characterized by abnormal retinal vascular development (so-called retinal telangiectasis)” → “so-called retinal telangiectasis” = **Specific Disease** (alias of Coats disease). “abnormal retinal vascular development” remains **Disease Class** (Section 1.2).
0061	
0062	- **(b) Name-basis deficit.** The phrase is the prepositional complement of a state noun (“deficiency in/of,” “deficit in/of”), the object is a gene, enzyme, or protein symbol that is the name basis of a recognized disease (the disease is named after the symbol, as with G6PD and “G6PD deficiency”), and the context is clinical rather than experimental.
0063	  - “a deficiency in G6PD among affected males” → “deficiency in G6PD” = **Specific Disease** (Example 40).
0064	  - A protein whose own name was derived from a disease does not qualify. Norrin is named after Norrie disease, so “deficiency of norrin” does not trigger this rule.
0065	
0066	**Negative constraints.**
0067	- A generic condition that does not co-refer with a named disease keeps the label assigned by Sections 1.1 and 1.2 (usually **Disease Class**).
0068	- A parenthetical that adds descriptive detail (location, laterality, severity, or an additional finding) without naming the disease does not trigger (a). For example, “(exudative retinal detachment)” after the consequence clause of the Coats sentence remains **Disease Class**.
0069	- A restating parenthetical attached directly to a head Specific Disease name is excluded under Section 5.1 and is not an alias under (a). The title parenthetical in “Coats’ disease of the retina (unilateral retinal telangiectasis)” therefore stays untagged (Example 21). Marker-introduced aliases in the body are still tagged.
0070	- A sign or symptom in a coordinated presentation list (e.g., “ichthyosis, mental retardation, spasticity, and deficient activity of fatty aldehyde dehydrogenase”) is not upgraded to **Specific Disease** by this section. It keeps whatever label Sections 1.1 and 1.2 assign: **Disease Class** for feature terms such as “mental retardation” (Example 29), or O if those sections leave it untagged. The coordinated-list items remain subject to the guideline-consistency review described in Section 5.2. Gold labels that assign **Specific Disease** to such signs are flagged for gold review and are not used to train this rule.
0071	- The head is nosological, or the phrase carries an explicit nosological marker. Apply the standard criteria in Sections 1.1 and 1.2.
0072	- The symbol is not the name basis of a recognized disease. For example, “deficient activity of fatty aldehyde dehydrogenase” in SLS and “deficiency of norrin” do not qualify. Deficits produced by a somatic or acquired change remain under Section 1.2 (Example 28).
0073	- The state is an assay result or experimental measurement, even when the object is a name-basis symbol (e.g., “enzyme deficiency in G6PD A-” from a bacterial expression study). See Example 30 and “What NOT to Annotate,” item 7.
0074	- Span boundaries of an inborn enzyme-deficit span are governed by Section 5.2. This section supplies only the label.
0075	
0076	#### 1.4 Diagnostic-Target Precedence for Generic-Head Disease Spans
0077	
0078	A noun phrase whose head is a disease-denoting umbrella noun (e.g., “disease,” “disorder,” “condition,” “cancer,” “tumor,” “neoplasm”), narrowed by an adjectival modifier or a prepositional complement, is labeled **Specific Disease** when it names the entity that the sentence diagnoses, differentiates, or defines. This applies when the span is either:
0079	
0080	- **(a) a differential target**, i.e., the object of a differential relation (“must be distinguished from,” “rule out,” “differential diagnosis of”); or
0081	- **(b) a defining criterion**, i.e., the single defining criterion of a named disorder introduced in the same sentence, or the defect the document sets out to identify for that named disorder.
0082	
0083	The head-noun-plus-modifier pattern alone must not trigger **Disease Class**.
0084	
0085	**Does not apply when:**
0086	1. The span carries an explicit nosological marker (an eponym with a disease-type noun, or an acronym). It is **Specific Disease** under Section 1.1 regardless, so this section adds nothing.
0087	2. The span is a generic symptom, sign, or finding reported as an observation, and is neither the differential target nor the sole defining criterion.
0088	3. The span is a hypothesized or proposed causal mechanism being tested or explained rather than the diagnostic identity. The default label stands.
0089	4. The span belongs to a class-membership or enumerating frame (“is an autosomal recessive disorder,” “is a syndromal X-linked mental retardation,” “such as,” “types of”). These are **Disease Class** under Section 1.1 (Examples 10 and 12).
0090	5. The span is one member of a coordinated presentation list of signs or features (Section 5.2).
0091	6. The head is a state, sign, abnormality, deficiency, process, or feature noun (e.g., “abnormal retinal vascular development,” “mental retardation”). These remain under Section 1.2 and rule 5 of “Principle Precedence.” Sequence-level entities are not annotated (Section 1.2).
0092	7. The span is attributive (a Modifier under Section 3.1 is applied first).
0093	
0094	Conflicting gold labels on spans that fall under these exclusions should be flagged for review.
0095

0098	### 2. Annotate **contiguous text strings**
0099	A textual string may refer to two or more separate disease mentions. Such mentions are annotated with the **Composite Mention** category.
0100	
0101	**Example**  
0102	> The text phrase “Duchenne and Becker muscular dystrophy” refers to two separate diseases. If this phrase is separated into two strings: “Duchenne” and “Becker muscular dystrophy,” it results in information loss, because the word “Duchenne” on its own is not a disease mention.
0103	
0104	**Coordinated disease disjunction.** A contiguous span in which a coordinator (e.g., “or,” “and,” “and/or,” a slash, “nor”) joins two or more disease terms is one **Composite Mention** when a conjunct depends on the shared head or modifier for its disease identity. This includes elliptical forms in which the head noun appears only once after the last conjunct (e.g., “breast or ovarian cancer”; “colorectal, endometrial, and ovarian cancers”). The rule holds in any syntactic slot: prenominal, prepositional complement, or attributive before a non-disease head such as a relational noun phrase (“family history”), a population, a case series, or a study noun. A span is a **Modifier** only when it contains exactly one disease concept.
0105	- Complete, separately headed disease names that each stand alone as mentions (e.g., “Wilms tumor, mesothelioma, leukemias, and breast cancer,” Example 8) remain separate mentions, because splitting them loses no information. This keeps the rule consistent with Example 8.
0106	- Distributed modifiers that each form a disease with the shared head are Composite (e.g., “adenomatous polyps of the colon and rectum,” Example 17).
0107	- The rule does **not** apply when:
0108	  - the coordinator joins non-disease elements that do not form disease concepts with the shared head (e.g., exposures, drugs, procedures, sample types, demographic terms, or tissue and treatment modifiers such as “smoking or alcohol exposure” or “liver and kidney transplantation”);
0109	  - the second conjunct lies outside the annotated span boundary (see “What NOT to Annotate,” item 6);
0110	  - the coordinated words form one fixed, lexicalized disease name;
0111	  - the items form a coordinated list of signs or feature terms in a presentation or definitional description. Those items are labeled individually under Sections 1.1 and 1.2 (see also Section 5.2).

0115	### 3. Annotate disease mentions that are used as **Modifiers** for other concepts
0116	A textual string may refer to a disease name, but it may modify a noun phrase (or may not be a noun phrase itself). In such cases, annotate using the **Modifier** category.
0117	
0118	**Example**  
0119	> Although this mutation was initially detected in four of 33 colorectal cancer families analysed from eastern England, more extensive analysis has reduced the frequency to four of 52 English HNPCC kindreds analysed.
0120	
0121	- **Annotate**:  
0122	  - “colorectal cancer” as **Modifier**  
0123	  - “HNPCC” as **Modifier**
0124	
0125	- **Attributive precedence (overrides the anchoring rule in Section 1.1).** A disease name is annotated as **Modifier** when it functions as a noun modifier of a non-disease head, such as a protein, gene, family, phenotype, cohort, population, case series, or kindred. This holds even when the name is an eponym or acronym that uniquely identifies a condition. The attachment test in Section 3.1 applies this rule.
0126	  - “Wiskott-Aldrich syndrome protein” → “Wiskott-Aldrich syndrome” = **Modifier**
0127	  - “SLS genes” → “SLS” = **Modifier**
0128	  - “G6PD deficient phenotype” → “G6PD deficient” = **Modifier**
0129	
0130	- **Family-history and cohort descriptors.** A disease named in a family history of relatives, or used to describe a cohort or case series, is a **Modifier** when it names one disease.
0131	  - “Both patients had a family history of breast or ovarian cancer” → “breast or ovarian cancer” = **Composite Mention** (Section 2; Example 35).
0132	  - “a family history of ovarian cancer” → “ovarian cancer” = **Modifier**
0133	  - “one hundred and seven ovarian cancer cases” → “ovarian cancer” = **Modifier**
0134	
0135	  A coordinated list of diseases in a family-history or cohort descriptor is a **Composite Mention**, not a Modifier. The Modifier label is reserved for a span containing exactly one disease concept. Splitting the coordinated list would leave a non-disease fragment (Section 2).
0136	
0137	- **Contrast.** When the disease name identifies a condition that an individual has (e.g., “a unilateral variant of Coats disease”), it is a **Specific Disease** (Section 1.1), not a Modifier.
0138	
0139	#### 3.1 Attachment-Based Labeling: Modifier vs. Specific Disease
0140	
0141	Once a span is identified as a disease name that is not coordinated (Section 2) and is not a class term (Section 1.1), choose between Modifier and Specific Disease by its syntactic attachment. Adjacency to a noun is not sufficient on its own.
0142	
0143	1. **Pre-nominal attachment → Modifier.** If the disease term is *directly* followed by a head noun, with no preposition, article, determiner, parenthesis, or participial clause between them, it is a pre-nominal adjunct and is labeled **Modifier**.
0144	   - “the ovarian cancer population” → “ovarian cancer” = **Modifier**
0145	   - “Wiskott-Aldrich syndrome protein” → “Wiskott-Aldrich syndrome” = **Modifier**
0146	   - “Most fragile X syndrome patients” → “fragile X syndrome” = **Modifier**
0147	   - “SLS genes” → “SLS” = **Modifier**
0148	
0149	2. **Referential attachment → Specific Disease.** If the disease term is not a pre-nominal adjunct, and it is (a) the complement of a preposition, (b) introduced by an article or determiner, or (c) a parenthetical acronym or abbreviation of the disease itself, it is labeled **Specific Disease** (referential mention).
0150	   - “a son affected by Norrie disease” → (a) = **Specific Disease**
0151	   - “the classical form of Coats disease” → (a) = **Specific Disease**
0152	   - “Sjogren-Larsson syndrome (SLS)” → (c) = **Specific Disease** for “SLS” (Section 6)
0153	   - An acronym that splits a single fixed name (e.g., “von Hippel-Lindau (VHL) disease”) is part of one mention (“What NOT to Annotate,” item 3).
0154	
0155	3. **Scope of the article and preposition tests.** An article, determiner, or preposition counts only when the disease term is the head of the phrase it introduces. In “in the ovarian cancer population,” “activation of Wiskott-Aldrich syndrome protein,” or “the Wiskott-Aldrich syndrome protein,” the article or preposition introduces a larger noun phrase headed by a non-disease noun. The disease term is then a pre-nominal adjunct inside that phrase, and step 1 applies. A parenthetical acronym counts only when it abbreviates the disease itself. “(WASP)” after “Wiskott-Aldrich syndrome protein” abbreviates the protein and is not a disease mention.
0156	
0157	4. **Exclusions that override step 2.**
0158	   - **Family-history and cohort descriptors** are **Modifier** (Section 3), even when the disease term is the complement of “of” (e.g., “a family history of ovarian cancer”).
0159	   - **Fixed proper names** with an internal preposition or article (e.g., “Gilles de la Tourette syndrome”) are one unit. The internal function words do not create a complement (Example 38).
0160	   - **Class-membership and enumeration frames** (“is an autosomal recessive disease,” “such as”) are **Disease Class** (Section 1.1).
0161	   - **State, sign, deficiency, and feature spans** are governed by Sections 1.2, 1.3, and 5.2. Step 2 never changes a **Disease Class** or **Composite Mention** label.
0162	   - **Coordinated spans** are governed by Section 2.
0163	   - Steps 1–2 decide only between **Modifier** and **Specific Disease** for a span already identified as a disease. They are not used to decide whether a token is a disease at all.
0164	
0165	5. **Parenthetical aliases.** A restating parenthetical attached to the head name of a Specific Disease span is excluded under Section 5.1 (Example 21). Finding-headed aliases introduced by a naming marker or alias gloss are handled by Section 1.3.
0166	
0167	Adjacency caution: a disease term followed by a noun that is separated from it by a preposition, article, parenthesis, or clause is not a pre-nominal adjunct, and step 1 does not apply.
0168

0172	For each sentence in the PubMed abstract and title, the locations of all disease mentions are marked, including duplicates within the same sentence.

0176	### 5. Annotate **minimum necessary span of text**
0177	Use the smallest span necessary to include all tokens expressing the most specific form of the disease.
0178	
0179	**Example**  
0180	> In the case of “insulin-dependent diabetes mellitus,” the disease mention including the whole phrase was preferred over its substrings such as “diabetes mellitus” or “diabetes.”
0181	
0182	#### 5.1 Nomenclature Boundary (Specific Disease Spans)
0183	
0184	- **IF** a candidate **Specific Disease** span is a disease-like head noun extended by qualifiers (laterality, inheritance or carrier status, extent, or severity), by a prepositional complement naming a molecule, gene product, or anatomical structure, or by a parenthetical or appositive that restates a disease already named in the text,
0185	- **THEN** tag only the compact, fixed nomenclature unit (eponym, abbreviation, or head-final compound) that the text uses as the disease’s name. Exclude the qualifiers and complement. Excluded elements are left untagged unless they are themselves a disease mention, in which case Sections 1 and 3 apply.
0186	  - “the classical form of Coats disease” → “Coats disease” (“classical form of” is excluded)
0187	  - “Coats’ disease of the retina (unilateral retinal telangiectasis) caused by somatic mutation in the NDP gene” → “Coats’ disease” only. “of the retina,” the parenthetical restatement, and “in the NDP gene” are excluded.
0188	
0189	- **Fixed-name test (negative constraint).** Do NOT trim when the full span is an established, fixed name, including:
0190	  - an eponym followed by a disease-type head noun, even with internal modifiers (e.g., “Sjogren-Larsson syndrome,” “Wiskott-Aldrich syndrome,” “Coats disease”);
0191	  - a head-final compound whose non-head element is an identifier or proper name (e.g., “Coats telangiectasis,” “G6PD deficiency”);
0192	  - a subtype or inheritance term that is part of a recognized concept or established name (e.g., “classical galactosemia,” Example 15). If no such concept includes the qualifier, it is trimmed under this section (see Example 16).
0193	
0194	  Also do NOT trim when the qualifier or complement is part of the established term rather than a case-specific description of an individual, family member, or defect.
0195	
0196	- Abbreviation parentheticals (e.g., “Borjeson-Forssman-Lehmann syndrome (BFLS)”) are not restating parentheticals. They are handled under Section 6.
0197	
0198	#### 5.2 Deficit Spans: Extension to an Enzyme or Gene-Product Complement
0199	
0200	- **IF** a span consists of a deficit-type head (a deficiency-type noun or phrase, such as “deficiency” or “deficient activity”) followed by a prepositional phrase introduced by “of” or “in” whose object names a gene, gene product, or enzyme, **and** the surrounding context frames that state as an inborn, heritable, or named clinical disorder (e.g., an inheritance pattern, a named syndrome, a germline or inherited mutation, or a “caused by” or “characterized by” clause),
0201	- **THEN** label the full span, from the head noun through the end of the prepositional complement, as one **Specific Disease** mention. Do not leave the head or the complement as O, and do not label the complement as a separate entity. A parenthetical acronym after the complement stays outside the span (e.g., “deficient activity of fatty aldehyde dehydrogenase (FALDH)”: “(FALDH)” is excluded).
0202	- **Generalization:** The trigger is syntactic (head noun + preposition + protein-name object), not lexical. It applies to any deficit-type head and any enzyme or gene-product object.
0203	- **Does NOT apply when:**
0204	  - the deficit head is a noun–noun compound with no preposition (e.g., “G6PD deficiency,” “glucose 6-phosphate dehydrogenase (G6PD) deficiency”). Label it under standard noun-phrase handling (Sections 1.1 and 5.1);
0205	  - the mention is a symptom or sign in a coordinated list of presenting features (e.g., “ichthyosis, mental retardation, spasticity, and deficient activity of fatty aldehyde dehydrogenase”). Do not extend these. Route them to separate guideline-consistency review. Pending that review, the feature-term rules of Sections 1.1 and 1.2 apply;
0206	  - the prepositional object is not a gene, gene product, or enzyme (e.g., “deficiency in African populations,” or a complement that is a nutrient, toxin, drug, or metabolite);
0207	  - the deficit is an assay result or transient experimental measurement rather than a condition of a patient or population (see Section 1.2 and “What NOT to Annotate,” item 7);
0208	  - the deficit is produced by a somatic or acquired change rather than an inborn or heritable one (e.g., “deficiency of norrin” after a somatic NDP mutation in retinal tissue). Such spans are handled under Section 1.2.
0209	- **Schema check:** This rule changes only the span boundaries of a **Specific Disease** mention. It introduces no new label, and it leaves the **Disease Class** and **Modifier** definitions unchanged.
0210	
0211	#### 5.3 Precedence Between 5.1 and 5.2
0212	Section 5.2 is the narrower rule and is applied first. Section 5.1 is the broader rule and explicitly excludes any span that Section 5.2 extends. When 5.2 applies, the prepositional complement naming the enzyme or gene product is kept in the span and is not trimmed under 5.1.

0216	### 6. Annotate all **synonymous mentions**
0217	Abbreviation definitions such as “Huntington disease” (“HD”) are separated into two annotated mentions (one for the long form, one for the abbreviation).  
0218	This applies to parenthetical abbreviations such as “Sjogren-Larsson syndrome (SLS)” and “Borjeson-Forssman-Lehmann syndrome (BFLS).” These are not restating parentheticals under Section 5.1.  
0219	A descriptive parenthetical that restates a disease name without being an abbreviation (e.g., “(unilateral retinal telangiectasis)” in Example 21) is not an abbreviation mention. It is handled by Section 5.1, and by Section 1.3 only when it is introduced by a naming marker.

0223	## What NOT to Annotate
0224	
0225	1. **Do not annotate organism names**  
0226	   - Organism names such as “human” were excluded from the preferred mention.  
0227	   - Viruses, bacteria, and other organism names were not annotated **unless** it was clear that the disease caused by these organisms is being discussed.  
0228	   - **Example**  
0229	     > Studies of biopsied tissue for the presence of Epstein-Barr virus and cytomegalovirus were negative.  
0230	     
0231	     In this case: “Epstein-Barr virus” and “cytomegalovirus” are annotated as **Specific Disease** category **only** if the context clearly indicates the diseases they cause, otherwise not.
0232	
0233	2. **Do not annotate gender**  
0234	   - Tokens such as “male” and “female” were only included if they specifically identified a new form of the disease.  
0235	   - **Example**  
0236	     > “male breast cancer”
0237	
0238	3. **Do not annotate overlapping mentions**  
0239	   - **Example**: The phrase “von Hippel-Lindau (VHL) disease” is annotated as **one single disease mention** (Specific Disease category).
0240	
0241	4. **Do not annotate general terms**  
0242	   - Very general terms such as “disease,” “syndrome,” “deficiency,” “complications,” “abnormalities,” etc. were excluded.  
0243	   - However, the terms “cancer” and “tumor” were retained.  
0244	   - These words are not annotated on their own or as unspecified umbrella terms. They can be part of a labeled mention when the span names a specific disorder (e.g., “C7 deficiency”) or a specified pathological state linked to a named disorder under Section 1.2 (e.g., “abnormal retinal vascular development”).  
0245	   - “infectious complications” remains excluded because it does not specify what is abnormal.
0246	
0247	5. **Do not annotate references to biological processes**  
0248	   - e.g. Terms corresponding to biological processes such as “tumorigenesis” or “cancerogenesis.”  
0249	   - **Exception:** A process noun modified by a deviation qualifier and linked to a named disorder is handled under Section 1.2 (e.g., “abnormal retinal vascular development”).
0250	
0251	6. **Do not annotate disease mentions interrupted by nested mentions**  
0252	   - Essentially, do not break the contiguous text rule.  
0253	   - **Example**  
0254	     > WT1 dysfunction is implicated in both neoplastic (Wilms tumor, mesothelioma, leukemia, and breast cancer) and nonneoplastic (glomerulosclerosis) disease.  
0255	     
0256	     Here, the mentions “neoplastic disease” and “nonneoplastic disease” are not annotated because other tokens break up these phrases.
0257	
0258	7. **Do not annotate assay results and experimental measurements**  
0259	   - Reduced activity, loss of function, or other deficits measured in experiments (expression systems, in vitro assays, or laboratory tests such as serum assays) are not conditions of a patient or population and are not annotated under Sections 1.2, 1.3(b), or 5.2.

0263	## Principle Precedence
0264	
0265	When more than one rule could label the same span differently, apply the rules in the following order. Each later rule explicitly excludes the spans handled by the earlier ones. Where a narrower and a broader rule overlap, the narrower condition is stated first, and the broader rule names it as an exclusion.
0266	
0267	1. **Coordinated disease disjunction → Composite Mention** (Section 2). A coordinated span in which a conjunct depends on the shared disease head, or in which the conjuncts are disease concepts that cannot be split without loss, is one Composite Mention in any syntactic slot, including family-history and cohort descriptors. This rule runs first so that no later rule splits such a span.
0268	2. **Attributive and attachment use → Modifier** (Sections 3 and 3.1). A single disease term that is a pre-nominal adjunct of a non-disease head, or that appears in a single-disease family-history or cohort descriptor, is a Modifier, regardless of whether it is anchored by an eponym, acronym, or gene.
0269	3. **Referential alias → Specific Disease** (Section 1.3). A finding- or state-headed phrase that names the disease under discussion through a naming marker or alias gloss, or that is a name-basis deficit complement in a clinical context, is Specific Disease. This overrides the default Disease Class or O label for that span only.
0270	4. **Deficit extension → Specific Disease** (Section 5.2). A deficit-type head + “of/in” + enzyme or gene-product object, in an inborn, heritable, or named-disorder context, is one Specific Disease span. Its exclusions (coordinated presentation lists, noun–noun compounds, assays, somatic deficits, non-enzyme objects) apply. Where rule 3 also applies, rule 4 sets the span boundary and rule 3 supplies the label.
0271	5. **State, sign, deficiency, and feature terms → Disease Class** (Sections 1.1, 1.2). Non-disease heads linked to a named disorder, and feature terms in definitional descriptions, are Disease Class. A patient-level role does not promote them to Specific Disease. Deficit spans that fail rule 4 (coordinated lists pending review, somatic deficits) fall here. Rules 1–4 are the only exceptions.
0272	6. **Disease-denoting heads → Specific Disease** (Sections 1.1, 1.4, 3.1). A disease-denoting phrase anchored by a proper name, eponym, acronym, gene, pathogen identity, or patient-level role is Specific Disease unless rules 1–5 apply. The attachment test in Section 3.1 chooses between Modifier and Specific Disease for such phrases. The diagnostic-target test in Section 1.4 can promote a generic-head span to Specific Disease, but never a state, sign, or feature head.
0273	7. **Span boundaries** (Sections 5.1 and 6). Trim per Section 5.1 unless the fixed-name test applies or Section 5.2 extended the span. Abbreviation parentheticals are separate mentions per Section 6.

0361	25. **“one hundred and seven ovarian cancer cases”; “a family history of ovarian cancer”**

0367	    - “G6PD deficient” = **Modifier**, because it modifies “phenotype.”

0369	27. **“Coats disease is characterized by abnormal retinal vascular development”**  
0370	    - “Coats disease” = **Specific Disease**  
0371	    - “abnormal retinal vascular development” = **Disease Class**, as a feature term in a definitional description (Sections 1.1 and 1.2). Its head is a process or state noun, so it is not promoted by Section 1.4, and it is not a Section 1.3 alias.

0374	    - “deficiency of norrin” = **Disease Class** (Section 1.2). It is not extended under Section 5.2 because the deficit is somatic, not inborn.  
0375	    - It is not **Specific Disease** under Section 1.3(b), because norrin is named after Norrie disease and is not the name basis of a disease. “norrin” is not tagged separately.

It is not upgraded by Section 1.3, and the items are separately headed, so they are not a Composite Mention. “(FALDH)” is not part of the span.

0386	    - “BRCA1 alterations” is a sequence-level entity and is not annotated (Section 1.2 negative constraints).

0389	32. **“…in the ovarian cancer population”; “one hundred and seven ovarian cancer cases”; “a population-based ovarian cancer study”; “ovarian cancer risk”**  
0390	    - “ovarian cancer” = **Modifier** in each. Each is a pre-nominal adjunct of a non-disease head (Section 3.1, step 1).  
0391	    - The article in “the ovarian cancer population” belongs to the larger noun phrase headed by “population.” It does not make the disease span a referent (Section 3.1, step 3).

0397	    - “ovarian cancer” = **Modifier**. It is the complement of “of,” which would normally give **Specific Disease**, but the family-history descriptor is an exclusion that applies first (Section 3.1, step 4).

0414	    - The internal “de la” belongs to one fixed proper name and neither splits the span nor creates a complement (Section 3.1, step 4).

0417	    - “hereditary retinal disorder” = **Specific Disease** (Section 1.4(a)). The generic disease head is the object of a differential relation, so it names the diagnostic target.

0420	    - “deficiency in G6PD” = **Specific Disease** (Section 1.3(b)). G6PD is the name basis of G6PD deficiency, and the context is clinical.  
0421	    - By contrast, “G6PD deficient” modifying “phenotype” = **Modifier** (Example 26), and “enzyme deficiency in G6PD A-” in an expression study is not annotated (Example 30).
