0001	# Biomedical Annotation Guidelines
0002	
0003	## What to Annotate
0004	
0005	### 1. Annotate all **Specific Disease** mentions
0006	A textual string referring to a disease name may refer to a **Specific Disease** or a **Disease Class**.  
0007	- **Disease Class**: Mentions that could be described as a family of multiple specific diseases.  
0008	- **Specific Disease**: Mentions that can be linked to one specific definition that does not include further categorization.
0009	
0010	**Example**  
0011	> Diastrophic dysplasia is an autosomal recessive disease characterized by short stature, very short limbs and joint problems that restrict mobility.
0012	
0013	- **Annotate**:  
0014	  - “Diastrophic dysplasia” as **Specific Disease**  
0015	  - “autosomal recessive disease” as **Disease Class**
0016	
0017	**1a. Disease-defining state complements** *(provisional: pending annotation review)*  
0018	This is the narrower condition, so it is stated first. A noun phrase is annotated **Specific Disease** when its head is a state-denoting noun, it is the direct complement of an etiologic or disease-characterizing predicate, and that predicate attributes the state as the defining feature of a single named disease. The state-denoting head is one of the following:
0019	- a deficiency or deficient-activity noun taking an enzyme or gene-product prepositional complement, or
0020	- a clinical sign noun, excluding phenotypic, cognitive, or symptom-trait labels (these are governed by §1b(a)).
0021	
0022	Qualifying predicates include “caused by,” “characterized by,” “due to,” “responsible for,” and “presenting with.” The span covers the whole head-plus-complement construction and excludes any trailing parenthetical acronym.
0023	
0024	Not applied (the mention keeps its current label):
0025	- (a) The state noun is a descriptive phenotype or downstream consequence, including complements of “results in,” “leads to,” or “is associated with.”
0026	- (b) The state noun is one member of a coordinated list of co-occurring findings (for example, “ichthyosis, mental retardation, spasticity, and deficient activity of …”), even when the list is attributed to a named disease. List members are not promoted individually.
0027	- (c) The governing predicate is a measurement, assay, or mechanism verb.
0028	- (d) The clause does not identify a nameable disease as the subject or governing noun.
0029	- (e) The state noun is a premodifier within a gene or protein designation (see §3).
0030	
0031	The default is the current label: non-disease state nouns remain O, and phenotypic or cognitive labels remain Disease Class. Because gold labels for this frame are inconsistent, this rule is not a hard constraint. Route candidate cases to annotation review (see Pending Annotation Review). Until a case is approved, §1b does not override its current label.
0032	
0033	**1b. Disease-process head nouns used as the patient's own condition**  
0034	A mention whose head noun denotes a disease process or pathological category (for example, a process-suffix nominal such as “telangiectasis,” or a generic infection or inflammation term such as “infection”) is annotated **Specific Disease**, not Disease Class, when the document uses it as the patient's own diagnosis or presenting condition. This means a singular reference, definite or bare, in a diagnosis clause or in a coordinated list of presenting findings. The label holds whether the mention is bare, lowercase, or broad in scope. A premodifying site, etiology, or organism adjective that narrows the head to one entity reinforces the Specific Disease label.
0035	
0036	Not applied (negative constraints):
0037	- (a) Phenotypic, cognitive, or symptom-trait labels (for example, “mental retardation,” “motor retardation,” “convulsive disorders”) remain **Disease Class**. A process or category term listed as one feature of a named disease (for example, “ichthyosis” in a list of SLS features) is treated as a phenotypic label. It is not promoted to Specific Disease, and its existing label stands.
0038	- (b) Generic, quantified, plural, or comparative references to a family of conditions (for example, “other causes of X” or “types of X”) are not forced to Specific Disease. Flag these for review, because gold labels may be inconsistent.
0039	- (c) Cohort, case, or population phrases (for example, “One hundred and seven ovarian cancer cases,” “the ovarian cancer population”) describe a group, not a patient's diagnosis. They follow §3.
0040	- (d) Biological processes (for example, “tumorigenesis,” “cancerogenesis,” “angiogenesis,” “vasculogenesis”) are excluded under “What NOT to Annotate” §5, even though their heads are process-suffix nominals.
0041	- (e) Downstream consequences and descriptive findings are not promoted. This covers complements of “results in,” “leads to,” or “associated with,” and parentheticals that describe a finding rather than naming the condition (for example, “massive intraretinal and subretinal lipid accumulation”).
0042	- (f) Generic terms such as “complications” are not disease-process heads (see “What NOT to Annotate” §4).
0043	- (g) A premodifying use, meaning a mention that modifies another concept, is labeled **Modifier** under §3, even when its head is a process or category term. This includes the premodifier of a gene or protein designation.
0044	
0045	---
0046	
0047	### 2. Annotate **contiguous text strings**
0048	A textual string may refer to two or more separate disease mentions. Such mentions are annotated with the **Composite Mention** category.
0049	
0050	**Example**  
0051	> The text phrase “Duchenne and Becker muscular dystrophy” refers to two separate diseases. If this phrase is separated into two strings: “Duchenne” and “Becker muscular dystrophy,” it results in information loss, because the word “Duchenne” on its own is not a disease mention.
0052	
0053	---
0054	
0055	### 3. Annotate disease mentions that are used as **Modifiers** for other concepts
0056	A textual string may refer to a disease name, but it may modify a noun phrase (or may not be a noun phrase itself). In such cases, annotate using the **Modifier** category.
0057	
0058	**Example**  
0059	> Although this mutation was initially detected in four of 33 colorectal cancer families analysed from eastern England, more extensive analysis has reduced the frequency to four of 52 English HNPCC kindreds analysed.
0060	
0061	- **Annotate**:  
0062	  - “colorectal cancer” as **Modifier**  
0063	  - “HNPCC” as **Modifier**
0064	
0065	**Context and population frames.** A disease name that premodifies, or is the complement of, a context or population noun (such as *cases*, *population*, *history*, *risk*, or *incidence*) is annotated **Modifier**. This holds even when the context noun carries its own premodifier, as in *family history of*.
0066	- “ovarian cancer population,” “family history of ovarian cancer,” and “ovarian cancer risk” are annotated as **Modifier**.
0067	- “prevalence of [disease]” frames are not added to this list pending review (see Pending Annotation Review).
0068	
0069	**Disease premodifier within a gene or protein designation.** If a disease-denoting span (full name or abbreviation) occupies the leading premodifying position of a multi-token gene or protein name, meaning it is followed by a gene/protein head noun and optionally by a numeral or bracketed symbol, label only the disease-denoting tokens **Modifier**. Do not label them O, even though the whole designation reads as a proper name. Leave the head noun, numerals, and bracketed symbols outside the entity.
0070	- Examples: “DTD gene” → “DTD” as **Modifier**; “ancient SLS genes” → “SLS” as **Modifier**.
0071	- Not applied:
0072	  - (a) The designation contains no disease-denoting token, such as a locus, organism, chemical, or purely alphanumeric gene symbol (for example, “G6PD A-”).
0073	  - (b) The disease-denoting span is standalone, not followed by a gene/protein head. It falls under Specific Disease or Disease Class (for example, “diastrophic dysplasia (DTD) in Finland”).
0074	  - (c) The span is not in the leading premodifying position.
0075	- Do not extend the Modifier label to the gene/protein head or to symbol tokens.
0076	
0077	---
0078	
0079	### 4. Annotate **duplicate mentions**
0080	For each sentence in the PubMed abstract and title, the locations of all disease mentions are marked, including duplicates within the same sentence.
0081	
0082	---
0083	
0084	### 5. Annotate **minimum necessary span of text**
0085	Use the smallest span necessary to include all tokens expressing the most specific form of the disease.
0086	
0087	**Example**  
0088	> In the case of “insulin-dependent diabetes mellitus,” the disease mention including the whole phrase was preferred over its substrings such as “diabetes mellitus” or “diabetes.”
0089	
0090	**Subtype/state-head span extension.** When a Specific Disease name is the complement of an “of”-phrase, the governing noun is a subtype or state noun (a noun denoting a variety, stage, type, or form of a condition, or a deficiency state), and that governing noun carries an adjectival, ordinal, or numeric premodifier, the span runs from the first character of that premodifier through the end of the disease name. The premodifier narrows the most specific form of the disease, so it belongs to the minimum span. Determiners such as “a” or “the” do not count as premodifiers for this purpose.
0091	- Illustrative: “an atypical form of Duchenne muscular dystrophy” is annotated as one **Specific Disease** span, not “Duchenne muscular dystrophy” alone.
0092	- Not extended:
0093	  - the disease name is not the complement of an “of”-phrase (for example, a bare name in subject or object position);
0094	  - the governing noun is a context or population noun (history, risk, incidence, cases, family, population). Such frames are **Modifier** under §3;
0095	  - the head word is lexically fixed inside a proper disease name, so the whole name is already the mention;
0096	  - the governing subtype noun has no premodifier (for example, “a form of X”). The span is not extended.
0097	- This rule changes span extent only. It does not change the Specific Disease, Disease Class, or Modifier label boundaries.
0098	
0099	---
0100	
0101	### 6. Annotate all **synonymous mentions**
0102	Abbreviation definitions such as “Huntington disease” (“HD”) are separated into two annotated mentions (one for the long form, one for the abbreviation).
0103	
0104	A parenthetical or “so-called” alternative name that names the patient's own condition (for example, “so-called retinal telangiectasis” in a diagnosis clause) is a synonymous mention and is annotated under §1b.
0105	
0106	---
0107	
0108	### Precedence Between Overlapping Rules
0109	Where two rules could label the same span differently, the narrower condition applies first:
0110	1. §1a (disease-defining state complements, once approved) takes precedence over §1b. §1b(a) and §1b(e) do not override a §1a match.
0111	2. §3 gene or protein designations take precedence over §1b. A disease span followed by a gene or protein head is **Modifier**, not **Specific Disease**.
0112	3. §3 context and population frames take precedence over §1b. Cohort, history, risk, and population phrases are **Modifier**.
0113	4. §5 subtype and state-head extension changes span only. Labels are decided by §1a through §3.
0114	5. The “What NOT to Annotate” exclusions remain in force unless a rule above expressly overrides them. For example, the §4 exclusion of “deficiency” yields to §1a only when the §1a conditions are met, and never for a fixed disease name.

0118	## What NOT to Annotate
0119	
0120	1. **Do not annotate organism names**  
0121	   - Organism names such as “human” were excluded from the preferred mention.  
0122	   - Viruses, bacteria, and other organism names were not annotated **unless** it was clear that the disease caused by these organisms is being discussed.  
0123	   - **Example**  
0124	     > Studies of biopsied tissue for the presence of Epstein-Barr virus and cytomegalovirus were negative.  
0125	     
0126	     In this case: “Epstein-Barr virus” and “cytomegalovirus” are annotated as **Specific Disease** category **only** if the context clearly indicates the diseases they cause, otherwise not.
0127	
0128	2. **Do not annotate gender**  
0129	   - Tokens such as “male” and “female” were only included if they specifically identified a new form of the disease.  
0130	   - **Example**  
0131	     > “male breast cancer”
0132	
0133	3. **Do not annotate overlapping mentions**  
0134	   - **Example**: The phrase “von Hippel-Lindau (VHL) disease” is annotated as **one single disease mention** (Specific Disease category).
0135	
0136	4. **Do not annotate general terms**  
0137	   - Very general terms such as “disease,” “syndrome,” “deficiency,” “complications,” “abnormalities,” etc. were excluded.  
0138	   - However, the terms “cancer” and “tumor” were retained.  
0139	   - A “deficiency” or “deficient activity” noun is part of a Specific Disease only under §1a (provisional). It is never excluded when it is part of a fixed disease name, such as “glucose 6-phosphate dehydrogenase (G6PD) deficiency.”  
0140	   - “Complications” and “abnormalities” remain excluded even when preceded by a disease-process premodifier (for example, “infectious complications”).
0141	
0142	5. **Do not annotate references to **biological processes**  
0143	   - e.g. Terms corresponding to biological processes such as “tumorigenesis,” “cancerogenesis,” “angiogenesis,” or “vasculogenesis.” Process-suffix heads of this kind are not disease-process heads under §1b(d).
0144	
0145	6. **Do not annotate disease mentions interrupted by nested mentions**  
0146	   - Essentially, do not break the contiguous text rule.  
0147	   - **Example**  
0148	     > WT1 dysfunction is implicated in both neoplastic (Wilms tumor, mesothelioma, leukemia, and breast cancer) and nonneoplastic (glomerulosclerosis) disease.  
0149	     
0150	     Here, the mentions “neoplastic disease” and “nonneoplastic disease” are not annotated because other tokens break up these phrases.
0151	
0152	7. **Do not annotate downstream consequences or findings**  
0153	   - Complements of “results in,” “leads to,” or “is associated with” (for example, “a deficiency of norrin” after “results in,” or “lipid accumulation”) are not disease mentions unless another rule above applies.  
0154	   - Phenotypic and cognitive labels are Disease Class, not Specific Disease (see §1b(a)).
0155

0158	## Examples
0159	
0160	1. **“Insulin-dependent diabetes mellitus”**  
0161	   - **Specific Disease**  
0162	   - Prefer the whole string.
0163	
0164	2. **CDH1 mutations predispose to early onset “colorectal cancer.”**  
0165	   - “early onset” may or may not be part of the disease mention, depending on:  
0166	     1. If there is a UMLS concept specifying this as a separate form of disease  
0167	     2. If the annotator believes it should be included
0168	
0169	3. **Human “X-linked recessive disorder”**
0170	
0171	4. **Her fresh serum lacked complement-mediated bactericidal activity against Neisseria gonorrhoeae.**
0172	
0173	5. **“Huntington disease” (“HD”)**  
0174	   - The long form and the short form constitute two separate mentions.
0175	
0176	6. **C7 deficiency**  
0177	   - “This report represents the first cases of C7 deficiency associated with infectious complications.”  
0178	   - “infectious complications” is too general a term; C7 deficiency is a **Specific Disease**.  
0179	   - “Infectious complications” remains excluded: “complications” is a general term and is not a disease-process head (§1b(f)).
0180	
0181	7. **“colorectal, endometrial, and ovarian cancers”**  
0182	   - Considered one **Composite Mention** of several Specific Diseases.
0183	
0184	8. **WT1 dysfunction is implicated in both neoplastic (“Wilms tumor,” “mesothelioma,” “leukemias,” and “breast cancer”) and nonneoplastic (“glomerulosclerosis”) disease.**  
0185	   - Potential disease mentions include: “neoplastic disease,” “nonneoplastic disease,” “Wilms tumor,” “mesothelioma,” “leukemias,” “breast cancer,” and “glomerulosclerosis.”  
0186	   - “neoplastic disease” and “nonneoplastic disease” are **not** annotated because these phrases are interrupted by nested mentions.
0187	
0188	9. **Tumorigenesis and cancerogenesis**  
0189	   - These refer to processes, not diseases (§1b(d)).
0190	
0191	10. **“autosomal recessive disease”**  
0192	    - Refers to a family of diseases that can be passed down genetically → **Disease Class** category.
0193	
0194	11. **Large phenotypic variability with “convulsive disorders,” “motor retardation,” and “mental retardation.”**  
0195	    - These are “grey area” terms: they may not correspond to a single **Specific Disease**, so they are annotated as **Disease Class** category if appropriate.  
0196	    - Phenotypic and cognitive labels such as “mental retardation” remain **Disease Class** (§1b(a)).
0197	
0198	12. **Borjeson-Forssman-Lehmann syndrome (BFLS) is a syndromal X-linked mental retardation.**  
0199	    - “Borjeson-Forssman-Lehmann syndrome” = **Specific Disease**  
0200	    - “X-linked mental retardation syndrome” = a family of diseases (i.e., **Disease Class**)
0201	
0202	13. **Acute meningococcal pericarditis**  
0203	    - Exists as a separate concept in UMLS.
0204	
0205	14. **Acute Neisseria infection**  
0206	    - May or may not include “acute” depending on context.  
0207	    - When used as the patient's own diagnosis, “Neisseria infection” is **Specific Disease**. The organism adjective narrows the generic infection head to one entity (§1b).
0208	
0209	15. **Classical galactosemia**  
0210	    - Includes “classical” because it corresponds to a particular form of the disease.
0211	
0212	16. **Inherited spinocerebellar ataxia**  
0213	    - May or may not include “inherited,” depending on the annotator.
0214	
0215	17. **“Adenomatous polyps of the colon and rectum”**  
0216	    - **Composite Mention**
0217	
0218	18. **Fibroepithelial or epithelial hyperplasias**  
0219	    - **Composite Mention**
0220	
0221	19. **Stage II or stage III colorectal cancer**  
0222	    - **Composite Mention**
0223	
0224	20. **“so-called retinal telangiectasis” in “Coats disease is characterized by abnormal retinal vascular development (so-called retinal telangiectasis)”**  
0225	    - “retinal telangiectasis” is **Specific Disease**. The parenthetical names the patient's own condition in a diagnosis clause (§1b, §6).
0226	
0227	21. **“a deficiency of norrin” (complement of “results in”); “massive intraretinal and subretinal lipid accumulation”**  
0228	    - Both remain **O**. Each is a downstream consequence, so neither §1a nor §1b applies (§1a(a), §1b(e), “What NOT to Annotate” §7).
0229	
0230	22. **“characterized by ichthyosis, mental retardation, spasticity, and deficient activity of fatty aldehyde dehydrogenase (FALDH)”**  
0231	    - “mental retardation” is **Disease Class** (cognitive label; §1b(a)).  
0232	    - “ichthyosis” is not promoted to **Specific Disease**; its existing label stands (§1b(a)).  
0233	    - “deficient activity of fatty aldehyde dehydrogenase” keeps its current label (O). It is a member of a coordinated feature list (§1a(b)) and is routed to review.
0234	
0235	23. **“One hundred and seven ovarian cancer cases were analyzed”; “a family history of ovarian cancer”; “ovarian cancer risk”**  
0236	    - “ovarian cancer” is **Modifier** (§3; §1b(c)). The span is not extended (§5).
0237	
0238	24. **“DTD gene”; “ancient SLS genes”**  
0239	    - “DTD” and “SLS” are **Modifier** (§3, gene/protein designation). The head nouns “gene” and “genes” are outside the mention.
0240	
0241	25. **“diastrophic dysplasia (DTD) in Finland” and “the DTD gene” in the same abstract**  
0242	    - In the parenthetical, “DTD” is a standalone synonym and is **Specific Disease** (Example 5; §3(b)).  
0243	    - In “the DTD gene,” “DTD” is **Modifier** (§3).
0244	
0245	26. **“G6PD A-”; “G6PD B”**  
0246	    - **O**. These are purely alphanumeric variant or gene designations with no disease-denoting token (§3(a)).
0247	
0248	27. **“the high prevalence of glucose 6-phosphate dehydrogenase (G6PD) deficiency in African populations”; “alone is not sufficient to cause G6PD deficiency”**  
0249	    - “glucose 6-phosphate dehydrogenase (G6PD) deficiency” and “G6PD deficiency” are **Specific Disease**. “deficiency” is part of a fixed disease name (§4). “G6PD deficiency” is already labeled, and §1a does not change it.  
0250	    - “prevalence” is not a subtype or state governing noun, so §5 does not apply. The context-noun rule in §3 is not extended to “prevalence” frames pending review.
0251	
0252	28. **“G6PD deficient phenotype”**  
0253	    - “G6PD deficient” is **Modifier**. The span premodifies “phenotype” (§3; §1b(g)).
0254	
0255	29. **“an atypical form of Duchenne muscular dystrophy”** *(illustrative, not from the reference set)*  
0256	    - Span: “atypical form of Duchenne muscular dystrophy” as one **Specific Disease** span (§5, subtype/state-head extension).
